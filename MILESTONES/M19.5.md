# M19.5 — a run of bits, and the width that makes it a type

**Landed 2026-09-08** (`09ac09c`).

**HALF A MILESTONE, AND THE SPLIT IS THE AUTHOR'S.** PLAN §8's M19.5 is
"binary and hexadecimal" and claims two numbered paths;
`satellite.variable.binary` `1 6 5` is built and `satellite.variable.hex`
`1 6 11` is not. The author called it mid-milestone — *"Let's just do b1100 for
right now, THEN we will do hex after this"* — and the reason it was the right
call is §2 below: the shape of the type was decided four times over the sitting,
and every one of those decisions would have had to be taken twice if both radices
had been in flight.

## 1. What is built

**One numbered path and its four children**, all new on 2026-09-08:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| `satellite.variable.binary` | `1 6 5 (0)` | the type; the `(0)` is new with its children |
| `.to_number()` | `1 6 5 1` | what the bits are WORTH — `b1010` → 10 |
| `.width()` | `1 6 5 2` | how many bits were WRITTEN — `b00001010` → 8 |
| `.to_string()` | `1 6 5 3` | the characters `display` prints, `b` and all |
| `.as_number()` | `1 6 5 4` | the digits read as decimal — `b1010` → 1010 |

**And the seventh arm of `Value`.** `Bin` is a `shared_ptr<const bits::BitRun>`,
sixteen bytes against the 32-byte widest arm, appended at the end per
value.hpp's append-only rule. The 40-byte `static_assert` did not move.

***PLAN §8's entry calls these "the sixth and seventh appends" and that is off
by one.*** It was written on 2026-09-08 while M19 was in flight, and M19's `Fil`
took sixth. Binary is the seventh and hex will be the eighth. The half of the
sentence that mattered — *"the assert does not move"* — was right.

**`write(x)` `1 6 2 11` gained its second arm and no new spelling**, which is
the whole reason PLAN put this milestone directly after M19. Until it did,
`write(x)` accepted only a string and read as `write_line` with the newline left
off — the entry predicted exactly that and it was true for one day.

## 2. What was decided, and how many times

**This was a design milestone and PLAN said so**: DESIGN §8.5 specified the
literals and the width rule *and nothing else*, and listed five questions
"somebody has to write". Four of the five were settled by argument; the fifth
turned out to be already answered by code.

**The width is part of the value, and that is the only clause a representation
can silently break.** `b0010` and `b10` are two values, `b0010 == b10` is false,
and display prints what was written. *Every other fixture in the section would
still pass if a bit run were an integer with a length beside it* — so the three
that pin the width apart are the section, and the note in `tests/eval_test/bits.cpp`
says so where somebody deleting one would read it.

**The bits are `satellite.variable.bool`, one per bit, and the author asked for
a measurement before agreeing to it** — *"you may have to run a test and see if
you can store the binary numbers as bool's, maybe you can if it's not too much
memory"*. Taken, one million bits:

| | bytes |
|---|---|
| `std::vector<bool>` — the bit-packed specialisation | **125,000** |
| one byte per bit | 1,000,000 |
| one `Value` per bit | 40,000,000 |

**The worry was real and pointed at the wrong row.** A `satellite.variable.bool`
*is* the C++ `bool` in this tree — value.hpp's variant names it as the second
arm and the language has no other — so "store them as bools" and
`std::vector<bool>` are the same instruction, and the packing makes it the
cheapest option rather than the dearest. The 320× row is what "a vector of
`satellite.variable.bool`" costs if each one keeps its own type tag, and that is
the shape the question was really about.

**Hex will be stored as bits too and converted at display**, the author's, for
the reason that makes it exact: a hex digit is four bits and the round trip
loses nothing.

### 2.1 `to_number` and `as_number` were reversed, twice, and the second reversal is the one with an argument under it

The two conversions went in as `to_number()` → 10 and `as_number()` → 1010; the
author swapped them — *"b1010 'as a number' is 10, and b1010 'to a number' is
1010"* — then asked which way it should be, and swapped them back.

**What settled it was not English.** Both readings of "as a number" are natural,
which is why leaning on them produced two answers. The two arguments that do not
depend on taste:

- **`to_` is already this language's conversion verb.**
  `satellite.variable.string.to_number` `1 6 1 13` turns `"42"` into 42 and
  `satellite.variable.number.to_string` `1 6 4 6` goes back, so a `to_` row
  answers the same value wearing the other type. Every other language answers 10
  when asked for an integer out of `1010` base two.
- **The surprising answer must not sit behind the expected name.** A reader of
  `b1010.to_number()` expects 10; a row that quietly answered 1010 would be a
  wrong answer that looks right, which is DESIGN §1.1 in the one place it is
  hardest to notice.

**What the flip cost, and what stopped it costing more.** The C++ functions were
first named `to_number` and `as_number` to mirror the two rows, so the first swap
was a rewiring rather than an edit. They are now `value_of()` and
`digits_as_number()` — named for what they compute — and `bits_methods.cpp` is
the only place the two vocabularies meet. bits.hpp carries the reason where the
next person would otherwise re-introduce the mirror.

### 2.2 A method needs a number; an operator does not

PLAN asked for **"two numbered paths and no third"**, and the four rows above
sit *under* `1 6 5` rather than beside it. That line is what made it possible to
answer the plan's questions without minting anything: `+`, `[` and the shifts
are operators and cost no path number at all.

**So M19.5 built none of them, deliberately**, and DESIGN §8.5 now records each
as open with the argument that will decide it. The author's sketch for `+` — one
operator for concatenation and a second, `!!`, for arithmetic addition — is
written down there rather than built, because arithmetic on a width-carrying
value needs a carry rule and an answer-width, which is precisely the sentence
this milestone exists to not write in a margin.

**Asked and answered by argument:** `b1010 == b00001010` is false (the width);
`b1010 == xF` will be false when hex lands (different arms never compare equal,
which §8's no-conversion model already decided).

**Asked and answered by the code that was already there:** *which of the two the
`.satc` writes*. SATC §3's "literals stay literal" governs it and
`write_expressions.cpp` already printed the token verbatim — `b1010` — with the
`b` doing the work of saying which type. Nothing had to change, and the question
was only open because nobody had looked.

### 2.3 `write(x)` refuses a width that is not a multiple of eight

A file is made of bytes and half a byte cannot be written. Padding to the next
byte and left-aligning in the last one both write bits the program never wrote,
so the refusal is the only answer that invents nothing — and it is one a program
can act on, `width()` being the question it would ask. Writing the *text* is
spelled `write(x.to_string())`, out loud.

**And the bytes are the bytes.** `write(b0100000101000010)` puts `41 42` and not
the seventeen characters of the literal, which is the difference that would be
invisible until something read the file back.

## 3. What building it found

**A method on a literal does not compile, and the entry predicted it.** PLAN
§8's M19.5 entry carries two working notes "to know before writing a line of
it"; the first is that a selector folds only through a declared name
(WORD_NUMBERS §1.5's one hop), and that *"this milestone will feel it more than
most, because a conversion verb is exactly the shape people chain"*.

**It was right, and it was found the way the note says it would be** — not by
reading the note, but by eight test fixtures failing at once, every one of them
a method called on `b1010` directly. `example/binary.satl` hit it twice more,
including on `run.width().to_string()`, which is the chain the note names.

The fixtures now name the receiver, **and one of them pins the refusal** so the
rule is proved rather than worked around. No milestone owns loosening it and
this one did not try.

**A `satellite.variable.bool` has no `to_string()`.** Found writing the example,
which wanted to join `b1010 == b00001010`'s answer onto a sentence and could
not. It is a real gap and it is one type over; the example displays the bool
instead and says why on the line above it.

**WORD_NUMBERS §2.2 used this very node as the example for a rule, and the
example was wrong about the reason.** The paragraph said `1 6 5` has no `(0)`
"because nothing calls it with no arguments — it is a type name, and the number
is the whole of it". But `string` `1 6 1 0`, `file` `1 6 2 0`, `time` `1 6 3 0`,
`number` `1 6 4 0`, `thread` `1 6 13 0` and `variant` `1 6 14 0` are all type
names and all carry one. **A type name's `(0)` is the BARE SHAPE** — a declared
name holding nothing, `satellite.variable.binary bits` with no `=` after it —
and binary had none because it had no children to hang them under. The rule did
not change; §2.2 now states it in the form that covers both kinds of word.

## 4. What proves it

**`tests/eval_test/bits.cpp`, fifteen checks**, and the three that matter most
are the ones an integer representation would fail. The mutations:

- **Trim a leading zero anywhere** in `satellite_bits/` — `b00001010` displays as
  `b1010`, `width()` answers 4, and `b00001010 == b1010` becomes true. Three
  checks, and DESIGN §8.5's whole claim is gone.
- **Swap `value_of` and `digits_as_number`** in `bits_methods.cpp` — the two
  conversion checks fail, which is the fixture pair standing in for §2.1's
  decision.
- **Drop a row from the install loop** — the count check fails: 41 handlers, up
  from 37, and the number is there so a row cannot vanish quietly. *That check
  is not in `bits.cpp`* — it is the one `section_scalars`, `section_variant` and
  `section_containers` already each carry over the summed install, and the four
  new rows moved all three. A count nobody can add to without touching three
  files is the point of writing it three times.
- **Accept a ragged width in `to_bytes`** — `file_test` still passes, because it
  does not know about bits; the check that fails is the refusal fixture here.

**`help_lines/verify.py` runs 173 worked examples against `./satl` and all 173
pass**, six of them new. The help entries are not prose about the type — they
are programs that were run.

**`nodes.tsv` was re-measured rather than typed.** The five new rows were written
by hand to get the tree building, then the enumerator was rebuilt against the new
objects and its output diffed against them: **identical**. So the `H` marks, the
arities and the receiver bindings in `help_lines/nodes.tsv` are what the dispatch
table actually holds.

**`make startup`: nothing regressed.** Every row within noise of the baseline,
both sides `STATIC=full`. The measurement is PLAN §9's ask; `make_support/startup.rows`
is unchanged because there is nothing to record.

## 5. What is owed

**`satellite.variable.hex` `1 6 11`**, which is the other half of PLAN §8's
M19.5 and the next thing to build. It inherits everything §2 settled — the
width rule, the equality rule, the four verbs, the byte-alignment refusal — and
adds one question of its own that binary did not have to answer: whether
`x00FF[0]` indexes a hex digit or a bit. Stored as bits and converted at
display, per the author.

**The operators**, all open and all recorded in DESIGN §8.5 with the argument
that will decide them: `+` and the author's `!!`, indexing, and the shifts.
None of them needs a word number, which is why none of them had to be settled
here.
