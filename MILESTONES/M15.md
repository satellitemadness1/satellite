# M15 — `satellite.variable.float`, the rounding rule, and the retune

**Landed 2026-09-04** (`PENDING`) — the same day as M13 and M14, behind them
the same sitting.

[PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) §8's M15 entry is the specification and
[DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) §8.6 is the type: a `satellite.variable.bool` and
two `satellite_number`s, the left half exact and unbounded, the right half
bounded and the only place the language ever rounds. This is a **review and
not a plan**: what was decided on the way, what building it found, and what
it left open.

**The milestone carried one blocker — the rounding rule — and the author
delegated it**, in the words "if there is anything blocking M15, look through
the documentation and the code and figure out the best course of action". §2.1
is the decision and the argument; it is M12 §2.1's shape, because the answer
turned out the same way: the tree had already taken the decision, and the
milestone's job was to notice.

## 1. What the milestone was

- **The float** — [src/satellite_float/](../src/satellite_float/), four
  files and an internal header. `Float` holds §8.6's `(positive, L, R)` with
  the three invariants held by construction: every road in runs through
  `from_number` or `assemble`, both normalize, and the default constructor is
  a positive zero — §8.6's zero-init footgun made unreachable through any
  constructor. The four operations are composition over the exact join:
  `to_number()` loses nothing, M8's signed arithmetic does the case analysis
  §8.6 spells out, and `from_number()` splits the answer back — so the sign
  is INHERITED, written once in `satellite_number/`, exactly as PLAN §8
  promised. The module touches Number's public surface only
  (`float_internal.hpp` carries the claim and `065-tests.mk`'s float_test
  link list is the linker checking it on every build).
- **The seventh arm** —
  [src/satellite_value/value.hpp](../src/satellite_value/value.hpp): `Flo`,
  a `shared_ptr<const Float>` appended after `Time`, sixteen bytes against
  the 32-byte widest arm, the third append and the third that cost nothing.
  The 40-byte assert did not move. `holding` answers "float", and
  `holds("float")` loosened from a refusal into an answer — the variant file
  note's promise, kept exactly as written.
- **The evaluator** — a float on either side makes an operation a float
  operation: `op_binary` promotes the number exactly (§8.6's conversion,
  "exact and always succeeds"), `+` `-` `%` exact, `*` `/` rounding the
  right half at `policy().float_digits`, negation one bool. `op_to_float` is
  the declaration's conversion — compiled only where the program wrote
  `satellite.variable.float`, shared by VarDecl and both Assign arms
  (`into_declared`), a store everywhere else staying checkless per M12 §2.1.
- **The three that waited** — `power` `1 6 4 10`, `truncate` `1 6 4 13`,
  `sqrt` `1 6 4 14` answer. Power and sqrt answer a FLOAT at every row —
  §8.6's result-type argument — with the exact rows exact (`2 ^ 3` is
  `8.0`, repeated squaring driven by the exponent's own bits) and the
  irrational rows correctly rounded (sqrt) or guard-argued (fractional
  power, §3.3). Truncate stays a number: toward zero, a float's left half.
- **The retune** — `evaluator/dispatch.hpp`'s `Assigners`, §2.2's decision:
  an assignment to a numbered language path is a handler-shaped write. The
  compiler's Assign arm gained the case between "declared global" and the
  M16/M26 refusal; `op_retune` looks the row up at RUN time (op_refuse's
  branch-that-never-runs argument, held for writes too); no row is S0724,
  S0721's write-side twin.
- **The dials behind the tables** —
  [src/satellite_system/](../src/satellite_system/), a leaf module the way
  the console is one: the four `1 14 2` reads as module constants and the
  four write rows, installed beside the console's and the clock's. Three
  dials read and store the running machine's Policy; `min_free_mb` reads the
  watchdog's own store and REFUSES its write with the reason (§2.2).
  `float_digits` joined Policy, `limits::float_digits()` and the config
  range row landed with the reader, and `satl --limits`'s fourth dial row
  stopped saying "stored; M15 reads it".

## 2. What was decided on the way

### 2.1 The blocker: round half away from zero

Truncate, half-up, or half-even — QUAD's determinism invariant demands A
rule, not any particular one, which is what made the choice delegable. The
argument, in the order it convinced:

1. **The tree had already taken the decision three times.**
   `Number::divide` keeps a guard digit and bumps at `5` — v1's behaviour,
   ported at M8, asserted by `tests/number_test/arithmetic.cpp`;
   `Number::round()` is documented "half away from zero"; and DESIGN §8.6's
   sign-magnitude rounds MAGNITUDES, with the sign decided before any
   arithmetic — so half-up and half-away are one motion here, and the two
   sites already agreed with each other.
2. **DESIGN §8.6 had already named the honest position** — "a default
   arrived by porting rather than by decision" — and PLAN's own paragraph
   about it ends in the tripwire: the test file asserts the behaviour "so
   changing it is a visible edit". Ratifying keeps every division the
   language has ever answered; number_test did not change by a character,
   which IS the ratification, checked.
3. **Truncate** would overrule `divide` at every call — `satl --number
   2 / 3` has ended in a `7` since M8 — for no property anybody asked for.
4. **Half-even's** virtue is bias cancellation over long accumulations, and
   it defends against a failure this type does not have: addition and
   subtraction are EXACT (§8.6's class 1), so the classic accumulation
   cases never round at all, while QUAD round-trips at six digits and
   prints at two — drift at place 34 is not a behaviour it can see.
5. **One rule, stated once, everywhere the language rounds.** The float's
   `R`, the number's division, `round()` — a language where two numeric
   types round two ways is two languages wearing one syntax.

DESIGN §8.6's "Still open" section now carries the rule and this argument;
the declined rules are recorded beside the taken one so the day one looks
wrong the losing argument is findable — M12's shape, again.

### 2.2 The retune: a handler-shaped write, into the Policy every read reads

PLAN §4.5.3's arrangement — *"the file seeds the namespace at startup and
the namespace is what everything reads afterwards"* — had no run-time half,
and M8's question ("a store the machine's Policy reads back, or a
handler-shaped write") turned out to be one answer and not two: **the
mechanism is a handler-shaped write and the MEANING is a Policy store.**
`Assigners` is a second small table beside `handlers[path_id]` — a column on
the hot table would widen every row for four entries — and the dial rows
validate and store into the running machine's Policy, which is what every
read already reads, so the next division simply observes the new count. One
mechanism, decided once, in front of `1 14 2 1` through `1 14 2 4` together,
exactly as the hand-off from M11 asked.

- **`max_depth` retunes through the machine** — `retune_max_depth`, a named
  method, because the machine caches the ceiling for the hot compare in
  `room()` and a bare field write would leave the cache stale (§3.5).
- **`min_free_mb`'s write is refused with its reason** (S0720): the watchdog
  is a detached thread reading the seeded store once a second, and a live
  handoff to it is a synchronization design of its own — a torn read there
  kills a healthy process, the worst thing a watchdog can do. The row says
  so rather than letting S0724 imply the path means nothing; its READ
  answers, and answers **nothing** when the dial is unset, because an unset
  floor means "not watched" and a `0` would be a number claiming otherwise —
  M12's askable state, earning its keep.
- **S0724 lives in the 72x decade** because it is about a program being
  EARLY rather than wrong: which paths accept a write is asked at run time,
  so the answer can widen row by row without the compiler moving.

### 2.3 The smaller decisions, each overrulable where it sits

- **The two numeric arms compare as values, in `==` and in `<` alike**
  (value.cpp, operations.cpp). Arithmetic promotes exactly, so `x == 0.85`
  after `x * 0.85` must not be a condition no program can satisfy — and
  QUAD.md §3.3's comparators are all `if (a != b) return a > b`: equality
  and ordering disagreeing across the arms would quietly break strict weak
  ordering. Every OTHER cross-arm pair stays never-equal, unchanged.
- **Mixed arithmetic answers a float.** The type that carries a precision
  wins, and the program asked for it: there is no float literal, so a float
  in an expression is always one the program declared or computed.
- **The declaration converts; nothing else checks.** `op_to_float` is one
  extra op compiled only under a float declaration or assignment — not a
  test on the hottest store in the machine, which M12 §2.1 refused and this
  milestone kept refusing. A non-numeric initialiser stops there with S0713.
- **`0 ^ 0` is 1** — the empty product, x⁰ = 1 with no exception carved in.
  `0 ^ negative` is S0601's own fact (1 over 0). `sqrt` of a negative is
  S0602.
- **A negative base under a fractional exponent is refused whole** (S0603),
  including the cases with a real answer: `(-32) ^ 0.2` is −2, but a
  fractional decimal exponent's root exists or not with the reduced form of
  its written fraction, and an answer that comes and goes with a SPELLING is
  §1.1's cleverness spent on the implementation. The refusal can loosen; a
  wrong answer could not have been taken back.
- **Integer-exponent power is exact and grows** — the §8.6 table's own word
  ("exact, and grows"), with the bound left to the float multiplication
  where §8.6 put it. The tension with class 2's "rounding is a policy" is
  recorded here deliberately: the table won because a result that is exactly
  representable and silently rounded is behind-the-back.
- **A float always prints its point** — `4.0`, never `4` — so the console
  never shows a float wearing a number's clothes. And its renderer writes
  the zeros out where Number's own would switch to scientific notation,
  because `1e+40.5` reads as nonsense (§3.4).
- **`float_digits` defaults to 34, beside `division_digits`' 34** — both are
  "the default width of an inexact result", and two arbitrary constants
  would be two facts where the language has one. Its ceiling is INT_MAX and
  not UINT_MAX, a representation fact rather than a bound: a fractional
  place lives in a Number's int exponent, so a wider right half has no
  Number to be rounded in. limits.hpp carries both, S0807/S0808 enforce
  them at the file, and the run-time write validates the same pair.

## 3. What building it found

1. **Division and sqrt can be CORRECTLY rounded, not merely guard-digited,
   and cheaply.** A quotient's last kept place is settled by exact
   cross-multiplication (`q` against `a/b` IS `q·b` against `a`), a root's
   by exact squaring — so a tie is a proved tie and float_test's
   `1/8 at two places is 0.13` is arithmetic, not luck. The naive
   guard-digits-then-round shape double-rounds on adversarial tails; it was
   caught on paper before it was code, and the exact settle is *less* code.
   **Fractional power has no such single cross-check** — it is a chain of
   tenth roots — so its last place is guard-argued (`kGuardPlaces`) and the
   file says so out loud; §6 carries it.
2. **`limits_test`'s "rot first" check fired the day it was written for.**
   The fixture asserting `float_digits=99999999999` passes unbounded
   existed precisely so that "if a later milestone gives a dial a range and
   forgets the table, nothing notices" — the range arrived, the suite went
   red, and the fixture now asserts both new refusals. The mechanism worked
   pointing backwards: the check rotted, not the table.
3. **There is no float literal, and a fixture proved it by failing.** An
   eval_test fixture wrote `satellite.variable.variant box = 1.5 / 0.5`
   expecting a float; both operands are NUMBER literals, so the box held a
   number 3. Only a float declaration or a class-3 method PRODUCES a float
   today — which is a fact worth this line, because it is also the reason
   mixed promotion is safe: a float in an expression is always asked for.
4. **`Number::to_string` switches notations under the float's feet.** The
   renderer goes scientific when padding would outnumber information —
   `1e+40`, `1e-20` — so every digit-level helper in `satellite_float`
   parses both forms through one decomposition (`float_internal.hpp`), and
   the float's own renderer writes the zeros back out on both sides of the
   point. Found by asking what `sqrt(1e40)` prints; the answer is now
   `100000000000000000000.0` and a fixture holds it.
5. **The machine caches `max_depth`.** `ceiling_` is copied out of Policy at
   construction for the hot compare in `room()`, so the retune goes through
   named methods (`retune_max_depth` keeps the pair together) rather than a
   mutable `policy()` — a bare field write would have left the cache stale,
   invisibly, until the first program retuned a ceiling and recursed anyway.
6. **errors.def took three rows and the counting test counted.** S0602,
   S0603 (numbers, the class-3 facts), S0724 (the retune's refusal) —
   reporter_test's row count moved 93 → 96 and its history note carries the
   entry, per its own instruction to say so here: the S06xx block's own note
   ("a row added here for a message no site can send would be this file's
   argument turned inside out") is exactly why the rows arrive at M15 and
   not before.

## 6. What it left open

1. **The float type has no methods and no children under `1 6 10`.** A
   float-declared receiver's selector folds through a type with no rows, so
   `f.truncate()` is S0723 today — and the four named conversions back to a
   number (`trunc`, `floor`, `ceil`, `round`, §8.6's "the program names
   it") have no numbered float rows to be called through. Minting numbers is
   WORD_NUMBERS §2.5's, which is the author's; the mechanism costs a
   `child_named` fold and handler rows the day the numbers exist.
2. **Fractional power's last place is argued, not proved** — `kGuardPlaces`
   in float_power.cpp, with the note saying widening it is the whole fix
   the day a misround is found. sqrt and divide are proved.
3. **`min_free_mb`'s live handoff** — refused with the reason (§2.2); the
   synchronization design is undesigned, deliberately.
4. **The digit-level helpers decompose through `to_string`**, a string per
   operation, keeping satellite_number closed at the cost of allocation on
   the float's hot path. Unmeasured against any real program; the fix is
   named where it would go (a digit window beside `small_parts()`), and
   QUAD's decay loop is the program that will say whether it is needed.
5. **There is no float literal.** `3.14` is a number everywhere, converted
   only where a float declaration names the crossing. Whether the language
   wants a literal spelling is the author's question; nothing here needs it.
6. **The startup baseline rows were left as they stand** — §7's measurement
   says why.

## 7. What it cost, measured

`make startup STATIC=full`, 2026-09-04, **load 3.10 against a baseline taken
at 1.00** — every row up a uniform +0.05 to +0.11 ms, including rows that
never touch a float (`satl --version`, bare `satl`). **Attributed before it
could stack**: the parent commit was rebuilt in a scratch worktree and both
binaries raced at the same instant, best of 5×200 — old 0.946 / new 0.934 ms
on `--version`, old 0.970 / new 0.965 on `--number 1 / 3`. The delta is the
machine's load, not the milestone's; the baseline rows in
`make_support/startup.rows` therefore stand, and the honest number for satl's
growth is the binary: **3,577,632 → 3,640,208 bytes STATIC=full, +61 KB** for
the four new objects and the widened evaluator.

**Counted at this landing: 249 C++ files, 40,326 lines over `src/` and
`tests/` (167 files and 27,973 lines of it `src/`), `words.def` at 583 —
unchanged, no path was minted — `errors.def` at 840.** Thirteen new files:
five of `satellite_float/`, two of `satellite_system/`, five of
`tests/float_test/`, and eval_test's floats section. The suite gained
float_test's fraction of a second; nothing else moved.

2. **"`satellite.library.system.division_digits = 40` retunes a running

   two lines apart in one run; eval_test proves the mechanism with its own

   value from the same Policy the division reads.
3. **"And — the blocker — the rounding rule is chosen"** — half away from
   zero, §2.1, recorded in DESIGN §8.6 with the declined rules beside it,
   ratified rather than invented, and tests/float_test/arithmetic.cpp is
   its written form.
