# M21 — a piece of QUAD, running

**Landed 2026-09-11** (`PENDING`). Two commits; §6 has both.

[PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) §8's M21 entry is the specification, and
[QUAD.md](../QUAD.md) §4 is where the demand comes from — *"one whose done-when
condition is a piece of QUAD, running. Not the whole program — one mechanism out
of `mind.hpp`, chosen because it exercises floats, containers, sorting and
persistence at once."* Three documents independently named the same candidate:
**`Sky::decay` plus `Rack::draw`.**

This is a **review and not a plan**: what was decided on the way, what building
it found, and what it left open.

## 2. The decisions

### 2.1 `Sky::decay` — 40 of 40, against the double's 6

`sky.hpp:176` with `persistence` and `clamp01` (`quad_core.hpp:56`, `:64`),
over eight thoughts spanning the whole altitude axis, four ticks.
**example/decay.satl** is the program and it asserts rather than prints.

Refereed three ways, because two were not enough. The C++ `Sky::decay` was
driven on the same fixture, and then **an independent exact-decimal
implementation refereed both**:

| | matches the exact referee |
|---|---|
| satellite | **40 of 40** |
| the C++ double | 6 of 40 |

Worst double error 6e-17; worst satellite error 0. **The two rows where
satellite and the C++ disagree in the sixth significant digit are both exact
ties at that width** — `line-40` at tick 1 is exactly `0.5606625` and
`semicolon` at tick 2 is exactly `0.4995225` — and satellite is the side
holding the value. The double lands 1e-17 to either side and breaks the tie
whichever way its binary representation falls.

**That is QUAD.md §3.1's argument for the float type, arriving as a
measurement.** §3.1 reasoned from `sky.hpp:461`'s six-digit save that six
digits is all QUAD needs; what this found is that satellite does not have to
choose, because the arithmetic is exact and the ties are provable.

**The fixture was built to make the two interesting arms run.** `structure` sits
at altitude 1, where `persistence` is exactly 1 and the decay is a no-op — it
holds `0.1` through all four ticks — and `faint` starts at `0.02`, close enough
to `kAwakeFloor` to cross it, get zeroed, and drop out of the walk at tick 2.
Without the second one the compaction arm is never taken and the awake count
never moves.

### 2.2 `Rack::draw` — the claim splits, and only half of it is a comparison

`rack.hpp:51` and the wheel it turns, `quad_core.hpp:90`. **The done-when asks
for "the same numbers as the C++ QUAD on the same input", and for a mechanism
with a generator in it that is only meaningful where the generators agree —
which they cannot.** QUAD's is `std::mt19937` and satellite's is PCG. So
**example/draw.satl** splits its own claim and says so in its header:

- **The deterministic core is COMPARED and agrees.** All four annealing
  exponents — `0.15 + 4.5·(1−t)` — and **satellite's are exact where the
  double's are not**: `4.65` against `4.6500000000000004`, `3.3` against
  `3.2999999999999998`, and the same twice more. **All twenty wheel choices are
  identical**, five uniform draws at each of four temperatures.
- **The stream is DEMONSTRATED and not compared.** What QUAD needs is not a
  sequence but invariant 8, and `quad_core.hpp:77` says why: *"the ability to
  see it twice is the difference between science and staring."* The program
  seeds, draws five, reseeds with the same seed, draws five, and asserts the
  two agree — then seeds differently and asserts they do not.

**The sequence itself is deliberately NOT asserted.** Writing PCG's output into
an acceptance program would freeze a thing `satellite_random/random.hpp` keeps
replaceable on purpose — v1's splitmix32 stub is the receipt — and invariant 8
asks that the same seed replay, not that it replay any particular thing.

### 2.3 The seeded tier, and the registry chose its shape

**The blocker was one number and three unrelated properties sharing a
namespace**, which is `SCRATCH.md/MILESTONE_DRAFTS.md`'s phrase for it. All
three were measured on this machine before the author was asked:

- **No draw answered a fraction** — `fast(0, 0.999)` is refused by S0905, and
  `fast(0, 1)` is a coin flip.
- **No stream could be seeded**, so invariant 8 was inexpressible.
- **About 184 ms per draw** — 0.92 s for five `fast` draws — against a 90 ms
  QUAD tick that draws from the rack once and calls the generator nineteen more
  times. **One tick would have spent 3.7 seconds spinning.**

**The author settled the shape on 2026-09-11: a fourth tier word.** And then
**the registry settled the roster, which is the more interesting half.** A call
shape is keyed by its word and its ARITY, and there are no dotted node names in
`words.def` at all — only an alias may carry a dot, and an alias takes no
number. So `seeded` has exactly four slots and the one-argument slot holds
either a digit count or a seed:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| `seeded(seed)` | `1 7 13` | **the number PLAN reserved**, and it takes the one-argument slot |
| `seeded(min, max)` | `1 7 14` | the fractional draw, `.range` a second spelling |
| `seeded(min, max, step)` | `1 7 15` | and the step may be fractional |
| `seeded()` | `1 7 16` | a refusal by design |

**So `seeded(digits)` is not offered, and nothing chose that.** It costs QUAD
nothing — it draws no N-digit numbers — and the whole-number draw this tier
does need is `seeded(0, 9, 1)`, which the step shape already was.

**The fraction is a GRID and that is what makes it answerable.** S0905's refusal
is true as written — there is no uniform draw over the reals between 1 and 100 —
and it is about the reals. A grid is countable, and satellite already carries
the grid's spacing on the value itself: DESIGN §8.6's *"R's digit count IS the
precision"*. So the draw is uniform over `(high − low) × 10^float_digits + 1`
points, every one exactly representable, inclusive at both ends like every other
range in §11. **The dial is read off the MACHINE and not the config**, so M15's
retune reaches this draw the way it reaches a division.

**And the grid is the finer of what was asked and what the bounds carry**, never
the coarser. A caller asking for two digits between `0.125` and `0.875` would
otherwise be answered on a grid neither end sits on, and *"inclusive at both
ends"* would quietly stop being true.

**S0913 exists because S0901 could not be reused.** The bare-tier refusal names
*"a digit_count, min and max, or min max and step"* — and this tier has no
digit_count. A shared sentence would have told a program to write the one call
it does not have. S0911 refuses a draw from an unseeded stream, and **refuses
rather than seeding one silently**: a stream seeded by accident is the
determinism bug this tier exists to remove, and a silent default would be
indistinguishable from a working program until the day somebody had to replay
it. S0912 is the seed itself — whole and not negative, because it names a
position rather than a quantity.

**One surprise, recorded because it is one.** `seeded.range(1, 100)` answers a
fraction, where `fast.range(1, 100)` answers a whole number. It is an alias of
`1 7 14`, so it draws on the grid like everything else here — but the four
`.range` spellings no longer all mean the same kind of thing. DESIGN §11.0 says
so where a reader will hit it.

### 2.4 The float had no methods at all, and `Sky::save` is what found it

**`satellite.variable.float` was a leaf in the numbering from M2 until this
milestone.** The type landed at M15 with four operations, a rounding rule and a
dial, and nothing a program could *ask* it — and that is defensible, because
arithmetic and `display` are most of what a float is for.

**`Float::to_string()` had existed in C++ since M15 with exactly one caller**,
`satellite_value/render.cpp`, the display path. So a value a program could see
on its screen was one it could not put in a string (S0711 on `+`), write to a
file (S0713 on `write_line`), or compare as text. **PLAN §8's M21 entry assigns
`Sky::save`/`Sky::load` to this milestone** and describes it as round-tripping
164 doubles at six significant digits — and `sky.hpp:461` writes every one of
them with a bare `operator<<`. **The save half was unwritable while the load
half already worked**, because `"0.4995225".to_number()` answers.

**A round trip available in one direction only is what made this a number
rather than a note**, and the author settled it the same sitting: mint
`1 6 10 1`. What it renders is what `display` renders — a second spelling would
let a program's file and its screen disagree about the same value, which is the
drift DESIGN §4.6 exists to remove.

**satellite's round trip is now the more exact of the two.** QUAD's
`operator<<` writes six significant digits every save, and QUAD.md §3.1 makes
that its argument for how much precision the program needs. This one writes what
the value is, so `example/decay.satl`'s assertion is equality rather than a
tolerance.

## 3. What building it found

### 3.1 A container is passed by value, and DESIGN does not say so

**`Sky::decay` is `void decay(double)` and writes through `nodes_` and
`awake_`. A capsule cannot do that.** Demonstrated: `bump(l)` doing `l[0] = 99`
and `l.append(7)` leaves the caller's `[1, 2]` untouched.

**It is deliberate and it is not a defect.** `satellite_value/value.hpp` holds a
list as `shared_ptr<const List>` and says so: the two containers are *"BUILT,
THEN FROZEN … every mutation is a copy published whole through the receiver's
storage slot"*, so a value two slots see can never change under either of them.

**But it cites DESIGN §6.4 for that, and §6.4 does not state it.** §6.4 is
*"Methods are sugar"*: it writes the receiver out as the first argument —
`satellite.container.list.append(my_list, x)` — and never says whether that
first argument is a copy or an alias. **So the rule lives in a source comment
and the section a reader is sent to is silent on it.** Both mechanisms here bend
around it by handing back what they changed, which reads fine twice and is a
cost `mind.hpp` would pay on nearly every method.

**M26 has to answer the same question one level up**, for a spacesuit method
mutating its own fields, and that is where this stops being a transcription cost.

### 3.2 There is no `break` and no `continue`

DESIGN §6's only loop is the C-shaped `for`, and `words.def` has four statement
words: `if`, `for`, `while`, `else`. `sky.hpp`'s decay has two `continue`s and
both became nested `else` arms. **Cheap, and it reads no worse** — recorded
because it is the kind of thing a port discovers rather than a table predicts.

### 3.3 A float's precision is PLACES, not significant digits

**The only place either mechanism found where the double is the more precise of
the two.** `settle`'s urgency is a millionth, and `pow(1e-6, 4.65)` is about
`1.26e-28`. `float_digits` is 34 *decimal places*, so at that magnitude **six**
significant digits survive, against a double's fifteen.

DESIGN §8.6's *"R's digit count IS the precision"* is exactly this sentence read
from underneath. **It changed no answer here** — that weight is 1.9e-28 of its
wheel's total, so discarding it entirely would have moved nothing, and all
twenty wheel choices agreed anyway — and **QUAD's weights span 28 orders of
magnitude by design**, so the next mechanism to lean on a very small float may
not be so lucky. DESIGN §8.6 now carries it with the number attached.

### 3.4 `write_line` says "path" about something that is not one

`out.write_line(a)` refuses with *"`write_line` wants a
`satellite.variable.string` path and this one is float"*. **Its argument is a
line.** `satellite_file/file_methods.cpp:92` reuses `path_at` for it, and
`path_at` hardcodes the noun in its own refusal — correct for the five callers
that really do take a path, and wrong for this one.

**Pre-existing, found by writing `Sky::save`, and NOT fixed here.** The fix is
to make the noun a parameter of the helper, which touches six call sites in a
module this milestone otherwise does not enter — so it is written down rather
than folded into a commit about something else.

### 3.5 The draw program's first draft passed a clause that proved nothing

`example/draw.satl` asserts that a different seed gives a different run, and
**the first draft drew at temperature 40 and passed while proving nothing.** At
40 the exponent is 2.85 and `look` holds 79% of the wheel, so five draws come
out in urgency order whatever the seed is — a sequence that would have been
identical had the seed been ignored entirely. It draws at 95 now, where the
wheel is flat enough for the seed to be the thing deciding, and the file says
why at the line.

**And two mutations survived the twenty wheel choices**, found by running them:
moving the urgency floor from a millionth to a hundred-thousandth, and changing
the wheel's `r < w` to `r <= w`. Neither is reachable through the fixture —
`settle`'s urgency IS the floor, so the floor never fires, and no uniform draw
lands exactly on a cumulative boundary. **§2a exists because of that**: it calls
the two capsules directly with a zero urgency and with a zero first weight, and
both mutations now turn the program red.

### 3.6 The seeded tier put `handlers.cpp` 40% over the line rule

Adding four rows took `src/satellite_random/handlers.cpp` from 249 lines to
**419**, against PLAN §3's *"try to build for 300"*. **Split into
`seeded.cpp` plus a `handlers_internal.hpp`**, leaving 266 and 213.

**The split is by SUBJECT and the rule is explicit that this is the only kind
worth making**: *"a seam chosen to satisfy an arithmetic is a seam in the wrong
place."* The subject is real — `seeded` has no throwaway window, holds a stream
the program seeds rather than the kernel, and is the module's only door that
takes a fractional bound, where the other three differ from each other in
nothing but how long they take. `satellite_scalars/hex_methods.cpp` is the
precedent one directory over, split from `bits_methods.cpp` on the day the
second radix arrived and for the same reason.

**And the internal header is the shape PLAN §3 warns about by name**, which is
worth saying rather than discovering: it cautions that the first satellite's
splits are *"visible in the result, with headers named `eval_internal.hpp`
existing to hold what an anonymous namespace used to"*. That is exactly what
`handlers_internal.hpp` is. The rule's own answer is that this is acceptable
when the seam is a subject and not an arithmetic — so the file says which it is
at its head, and `install_handlers()` remains the module's one door.

### 3.7 A count assertion disagreed with its own message, by one

`tests/reporter_test/codes.cpp` guards how many rows `errors.def` has, because
*"a row DELETED is invisible to every static_assert in codes.hpp"*. M21 added
three — S0911, S0912, S0913 — and went to fix the number, which is when the
line turned out to read:

    check(kCodeCount == 117,
          "errors.def has 116 rows -- if that changed on purpose, ...");

**The comparison said 117 and its own sentence said 116**, and had since M20
moved the count by two and updated one of them. Nothing was broken by it: the
assertion is the comparison, so the guard held and only the explanation was
wrong — which is precisely what makes it worth writing down. **A count in a
message has nothing checking it, sitting inside the check written to catch
exactly that.** §3.8 is the same failure one directory over, in `words.def`'s
header, on the same day and from the same milestone. Both numbers are now
written once each and read together.

**And this is the third count guard M21 tripped**, after §5's three
`eval_test` install counts and `words_test`'s four. All of them are guards
against a row *vanishing*, and all of them caught a row *arriving* — which is
the property worth having and is nowhere stated as a design goal.

### 3.8 `words.def`'s own tallies were stale again, and its comment said they would be

The header block read `279 / 46 / 325 / 11` against a registry of
`290 / 46 / 336 / 11` — **M20's own numbers, and M20.md §2.1 says in its own
text that it took `kNodeCount` to 336**, so the block was eleven nodes stale
before the milestone that wrote it had finished landing. That is the fifth time,
and the paragraph beneath it had already predicted this one: *"the next
milestone to append will be the fifth to walk past it."*

**Found by counting rather than by reading**, which is the only way it could
have been: `grep -c '^SAT_NODE('` is the whole measurement. It now reads
`342 / 295 / 47 / 12` and it was counted that way. A count in a comment still
has nothing checking it — but `tests/words_test` now asserts all four of §2.2's,
so the block is derivable from a checked thing in one subtraction.

## 4. What it left open

- **`Sky::save`'s `operator<<` precision is not imitated.** satellite writes the
  whole value where QUAD writes six significant digits. That is strictly better
  and it means the two files are not byte-identical, so a satellite `.sky` and a
  C++ `.sky` of the same mind differ. Nothing needs them to today.
- **§3.1's by-value cost is unpaid and lands on M26.** Both mechanisms here hand
  back what they changed; a spacesuit method has a receiver to write through and
  the same rule to obey, and nothing has decided which wins.
- **§3.4's wording defect is written down and not fixed.**
- **THE SEEDED TIER REFUSES A FLOAT BOUND**, which is its sharpest edge and was
  found after it was built, by asking for a span of `10^99999`:
  `huge.power(99999)` answers a **float** (DESIGN §8.6's result-type rule) and
  every door in `satellite_random` reads the Number arm, so it gets S0904's
  *"wants two `satellite.variable.number` arguments"* — literally true, and odd
  against a draw whose whole point is fractions. **A fractional literal is a
  Number** (§8.1: `0.999` is exact), so everything a program types works and
  `seeded(0.125, 0.875)` is fine; what is refused is a value that reached
  float. `Float::to_number()` is exact and would close it in three lines.
  **Not taken here** — it changes the argument contract of all four tiers, and
  the other three have the same edge for the same reason.
- **The width check is on the UNSCALED span**, so the seeded tier's real
  ceiling is `MAX_RANDOM_DIGITS` less `float_digits`. A span between the two is
  caught one step further in, by `seeded_grid` answering false, and comes out
  as S0907 rather than a crash. **Reasoned and not exercised**: reaching it
  needs a hundred-thousand-digit literal, and the path is the one the three
  spinning tiers already share and `the_rows_answer()` already tests.
- **`satellite.system()` `1 22 0` and `memory.this()` `1 22 4 5 0` still
  refuse** — M20 §4's two, untouched here.
- **The whole of `mind.hpp` is still unwritten.** This milestone is two
  mechanisms out of 3029 lines, and QUAD.md §0's rule — both sides bend, QUAD
  bends more — has now been applied for real exactly twice.

## 5. What proves it

- **`example/decay.satl`** — 40 activations and the awake count asserted against
  exact decimal literals, plus the `.sky` round trip. Three mutations checked:
  `persistence`'s `0.85`, `kAwakeFloor`, and the rate. Two more on the round
  trip: writing the wrong column, and a header that becomes a ninth node.
- **`example/draw.satl`** — four exponents, twenty wheel choices, two weights
  outright, the wheel's two edges, and invariant 8 in both directions. Four
  mutations checked — the exponent constant, the urgency floor, the wheel's
  `r < w`, and the all-zero guard's `total <= 0` — and **three of the four are
  caught only by §3.5's §2a**, which is why that section exists.
- **`help_lines/verify.py`** — 271 worked lines run and passed. **Six entries
  written here and five of them exercised**: three that run, one marked
  `refuses` (`seeded()`), and the float's bare `(0)` row, whose example is
  empty because a type is not a call.
- **`tests/words_test`** — §2.2 against the registry: 302 rows, 298 numbers,
  4 aliases, 44 markers, all four moved together.
- **`tests/eval_test`** — twenty-two clauses over the seeded tier and the
  float's method, in `clock_and_dice.cpp`. **The seeded tier's clauses run
  FIRST in the section and one of them had to**: the stream is one per
  process, so nothing can unseed it, and S0911's refusal is only observable
  before the first `seeded(seed)` in the whole run. A clause that checked it
  later would pass or fail on what ran before it.
- **AND THREE INSTALL COUNTS CAUGHT THE NEW ROW, which is the guard working in
  the direction it was not written for.** `scalars.cpp`, `variant.cpp` and
  `containers.cpp` each assert how many rows are in the dispatch table after an
  install, and each says why: *"the count is here so a row dropped from an
  install loop cannot vanish quietly."* `install_float_methods` ADDED one, and
  all three went red on the same run — so the assertion that exists to catch a
  disappearance catches an appearance for free, and the three had to move
  together or the one that did not would be the finding.
- **`make nodes`** — `help_lines/nodes.tsv` re-measured rather than typed, and
  the measurement confirmed all six new rows at the mark and arity they were
  written with.

§2.2 moved 296 → 302 rows and 293 → 298 numbers; `kNodeCount` 336 → 342, and
the words digest `03e03c025294f677` → `ca4810a483e0d61c`, which invalidates
every cached `.satc` on the machine exactly as SATC.md §3 says it should.
