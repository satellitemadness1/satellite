--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SATELLITE 004: satellite.random
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Random whole numbers, in three grades: fast, normal and ultra. How many
numbers you give decides what you asked for -- one number is a count of
digits, two numbers are a min and a max, three are a min, a max and a step --
and each may be written out or be a variable:

    satellite.random.normal(20)            twenty digits: 0 up to twenty nines
    satellite.random.normal(1, 6)          a die: 1 to 6, both ends included
    satellite.random.normal(0, 100, 5)     one of 0, 5, 10 ... 100

    satellite.variable.number my_random_number = satellite.random.fast(20)
    satellite.variable.number my_die = satellite.random.fast(1, 6)

A count of digits is uniform over every number of up to that many digits, so
about one draw in ten of normal(20) prints 19 digits or fewer -- a leading zero
is not printed. That is what uniform means. A range includes both ends; a
step lands on min, min + step, and so on up to max, and max must be a value
the step lands on or the call is refused and names the last value it reaches.

The numbers are exact at any size: ultra(1000) is a thousand digits, and
normal(10 ^ 40, 10 ^ 41) draws between those two, every value as likely.

    satellite.include(satellite)

    satellite.capsule satellite.main()
    {
        satellite.variable.number die = satellite.random.fast(1, 6)
        satellite.console.display(die)
        satellite.console.display(satellite.random.normal(20))
        satellite.console.display(satellite.random.ultra(0, 100, 5))
        satellite.return(satellite)
    }

What makes the grades. Every call first draws how long it will take, inside
its grade's window, then throws away random numbers of the size it was asked
for until that long has passed, and only then answers:

    fast      500 to 1000 ms
    normal    1000 to 2000 ms
    ultra     2000 to 3000 ms

So a draw takes at least half a second, by design, and the length of any one
is itself random. A call that is refused never spins: every check comes first.

What the generator is. All three grades draw from one generator,
pcg512_k16384: the PCG generator pcg32_k16384 (pcg-random.org) at 512 bits --
a 1024-bit state, a table of 16384 words of 512 bits folded over its output,
1 MiB in all, seeded whole from the kernel. Every thread of a program has a
generator of its own, made and seeded the first time that thread draws, so
threads never share a stream and a thread that never draws costs nothing. It
is not a secret-keeping generator: PCG makes no cryptographic claim and this
page makes none.

The bare shape, satellite.random.fast(), draws nothing: a random number with
no width and no bounds is not a question with an answer, and it is refused
before the program runs, naming the three shapes. So is this, refused today:

    satellite.include(satellite)

    satellite.capsule satellite.main()
    {
        satellite.console.display(satellite.random.ultra())
        satellite.return(satellite)
    }

Refused when the line runs, before any spin: a bound or a step that is not a
whole number (there is no even way to pick from every number between two
fractions), a count of digits below 0, min above max, a step below 1, and a
step that misses max. satellite.random.seeded, which would draw the same
numbers from the same seed every run, is numbered and not built yet.
