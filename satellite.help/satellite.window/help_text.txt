
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SATELLITE 004: satellite.window
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

satellite.window is the family of words that make a window and the pieces you
put in one, each a satellite.variable.window. A piece is on no screen until you
.satellite.append it into a window, or into something that is in one:

  new(title, width, height)  new(title, subtitle, width, height)  button(text)
  label(text)  text_box(text)
  text_box(text, wide)  text_area(text)  checkbox(text)  switch()  slider(least, most)
  number_box(least, most)  progress()  choice(list)  row()  column()  grid()
  picture(path)  scroll()  frame(title)  split()  menu(title)
  canvas(width, height)  tabs()  one_of(list)

satellite.window.new("a title", "the words under it", 800, 600) puts a second,
smaller line of words under the title, in the window's bar. A width and a height are written
800, 600 or 800x600 -- the same two numbers, the width first.

Since 2026-10-11 every word a window and a piece have is written with satellite.
in front of it: my_window.satellite.open(), and at every link of a chain,
my_window.satellite.title("x").satellite.focus(). The old my_window.open() is
refused (S110), and the refusal names the new spelling. Your own names after a
window -- a widget's, a section's -- keep their plain period, and a word of two
or three parts takes satellite. once, before the whole word:
my_window.files.satellite.widgets.display.file("list", 100%, 100%).

A window makes widgets with .satellite.widgets.display, and a widget that holds
widgets makes its own the same way, each under a name in quotes that reaches it
after: my_window.satellite.widgets.display.file("files") is a file display --
the files of a directory, folders first, starting in your home folder --
reached as my_window.files, and
my_window.files.satellite.widgets.display.input("folder") is a one-line text
input across its top, starting with that directory, reached as
my_window.files.folder. A window's widgets go top to bottom, and so do a
widget's, above what it shows. my_window.files.satellite.directory("/tmp/")
changes the directory shown -- one that is not there leaves it where it was --
and .satellite.directory alone reads it.
my_window.files.folder.satellite.listen(a_capsule) runs the capsule on every
key typed in the input, and .satellite.listen() alone just listens;
.satellite.text is always what is typed.

SECTIONS lay a window out. my_window.satellite.section("top", 100%, 50%) is a
section as wide as the window and half as tall -- the window under its title
bar, its menu and any widgets made across its top -- reached as my_window.top.
A percentage is a share of what holds it, and a plain number is pixels:
.satellite.section("side", 300, 200). Sections fill the window left to right,
and one that does not fit starts a new row under the tallest before it: two
50%, 100% sit side by side, and two 100%, 50% are the top and bottom halves. A
section holds widgets and sections of its own, sized the same way --
my_window.top.satellite.widgets.display.file("files", 100%, 100%) fills it --
and a widget given no size in a section fills it. A share stays a share when
the window is resized. A window is laid out in sections or places pieces with
.satellite.append, one or the other, for now.

my_window.satellite.append(piece, across, down) puts the piece's CENTRE there;
400, 300 is the middle of an 800 by 600 window. A row and a column take
.satellite.append(piece), a grid .satellite.append(piece, across, down) as a
cell counting from 1. A scroll and a frame hold one piece, a split two, and a
set of tabs names each tab by its piece's .satellite.title("a name").
my_window.satellite.menu(a_menu) puts a menu across the top; a menu has
.satellite.item(a_capsule, "Open") and .satellite.separator().

A text box is 25 characters wide unless its word says how wide:
satellite.window.text_box("", 40) is 40 characters wide, and
satellite.window.text_box("", 100%) is as wide as the window it is
appended into, measured as it is appended -- 250% reaches past the window's
edge. A box given a percent goes straight into a window; in a row or a column
give it characters instead.

A window scrolls when a piece is appended past its edge:
my_window.satellite.scroll.vertical(1) and .satellite.scroll.horizontal(1) turn
each way on, (0) off, and each read bare answers which. A window starts as the
rows satellite.arguments.scroll.vertical and
satellite.arguments.scroll.horizontal say, true unless config.ini or a line at
the top of the file says false. A console has the same two:
satellite.help(satellite.console).

.satellite.text, .satellite.on, .satellite.value, .satellite.chosen,
.satellite.path and .satellite.title read with no brackets and set with them:
.satellite.text("hi"), .satellite.on(1), .satellite.value(50),
.satellite.chosen("red"). A progress bar's .satellite.value is a percentage,
.satellite.value(50%). There is no true to type yet, so .satellite.on takes 1
or 0.

A piece talks back by running a capsule, named bare and first:
.satellite.pressed on a button, .satellite.changed on anything a person can
change, .satellite.clicked on anything but a button or a menu; and on a window
.satellite.closed, .satellite.key, .satellite.every(a_capsule, 1000) in
milliseconds, and .satellite.ask(a_capsule, "sure?") and
.satellite.choose_a_file(a_capsule), whose answer is
its_window.satellite.answer. The capsule may declare nothing, the piece, or the
piece and its window. .satellite.press() is your program pressing a button
itself.

A window also has .satellite.open(), .satellite.close(), .satellite.focus(),
.satellite.resize(1024, 768), .satellite.fullscreen(1) and
.satellite.message("saved"). new puts a window up and goes straight on;
.satellite.open() waits until it has been drawn, and puts back a window that has
closed: its title, its size and its capsules come back, and what was in it
closed with it and is appended again. Every piece but a menu has
.satellite.width, .satellite.height, .satellite.colour("red") (or
.satellite.color), .satellite.background("#222228") and
.satellite.font("IBM Plex Mono", 12). A canvas draws with .satellite.line,
.satellite.box, .satellite.circle, .satellite.arc and .satellite.write;
.satellite.clear() and .satellite.save("a.png").

satl refuses a wrong argument count, a method no piece has and a capsule nobody
wrote before a line runs, and a method this piece lacks (.satellite.resize on a
button) when its line runs. With no screen, the first window word stops with
S730 NO_DISPLAY and satl exits 50.

example:

satellite.include(satellite)

satellite.capsule when_typed(satellite.variable.window the_box)
{
    satellite.console.display(the_box.satellite.text)
}

satellite.capsule when_done(satellite.variable.window the_button, satellite.variable.window its_window)
{
    its_window.satellite.close()
}

satellite.capsule satellite.main()
{
    satellite.variable.window my_window = satellite.window.new("a form", 400, 300)
    satellite.variable.window a_column = satellite.window.column()
    satellite.variable.window your_name = satellite.window.text_box("")
    your_name.satellite.changed(when_typed)
    satellite.variable.window done = satellite.window.button("done")
    done.satellite.pressed(when_done)
    a_column.satellite.append(your_name)
    a_column.satellite.append(done)
    my_window.satellite.append(a_column, 200, 150)
    satellite.return(satellite)
}

example, sections -- a file display in the top half, an input in the bottom:

satellite.include(satellite)

satellite.capsule satellite.main()
{
    satellite.variable.window main_window = satellite.window.new("window title", "version", 800, 600)
    main_window.satellite.section("section_name", 100%, 50%)
    main_window.section_name.satellite.widgets.display.file("my_file_display", 100%, 100%)
    main_window.satellite.section("bottom", 100%, 50%)
    main_window.bottom.satellite.widgets.display.input("folder")
    satellite.return(satellite)
}
