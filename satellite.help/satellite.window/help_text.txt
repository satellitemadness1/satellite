
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SATELLITE 004: satellite.window
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

satellite.window is the family of words that make a window and the pieces you
put in one, each a satellite.variable.window. A piece is on no screen until you
.append it into a window, or into something that is in one:

  new(title, width, height)  new(title, subtitle, width, height)  button(text)
  label(text)  text_box(text)
  text_box(text, wide)  text_area(text)  checkbox(text)  switch()  slider(least, most)
  number_box(least, most)  progress()  choice(list)  row()  column()  grid()
  picture(path)  scroll()  frame(title)  split()  menu(title)
  canvas(width, height)  tabs()  one_of(list)

new("a title", "the words under it", 800, 600) puts a second, smaller line of
words under the title, in the window's bar. A width and a height are written
800, 600 or 800x600 -- the same two numbers, the width first.

A window makes widgets with .widgets, and a widget that holds widgets makes its
own the same way, each under a name in quotes that reaches it after:
my_window.widgets.displays.file("files") is a file display -- the files of a
directory, folders first, starting in your home folder -- reached as
my_window.files, and my_window.files.widgets.displays.input("folder") is a
one-line text input across its top, starting with that directory, reached as
my_window.files.folder. A window's widgets go top to bottom, and so do a widget's,
above what it shows. my_window.files.directory("/tmp/") changes the directory
shown -- one that is not there leaves it where it was -- and .directory alone
reads it. my_window.files.folder.listen(a_capsule) runs the capsule on every key
typed in the input, and .listen() alone just listens; .text is always what is
typed.

SECTIONS lay a window out. my_window.section("top", 100%, 50%) is a section as
wide as the window and half as tall -- the window under its title bar, its menu
and any widgets made across its top -- reached as my_window.top. A percentage
is a share of what holds it, and a plain number is pixels: section("side", 300,
200). Sections fill the window left to right, and one that does not fit starts
a new row under the tallest before it: two 50%, 100% sit side by side, and two
100%, 50% are the top and bottom halves. A section holds widgets and sections
of its own, sized the same way -- my_window.top.widgets.displays.file("files",
100%, 100%) fills it -- and a widget given no size in a section fills it. A
share stays a share when the window is resized. A window is laid out in
sections or places pieces with .append, one or the other, for now.

my_window.append(piece, across, down) puts the piece's CENTRE there; 400, 300 is
the middle of an 800 by 600 window. A row and a column take .append(piece), a
grid .append(piece, across, down) as a cell counting from 1. A scroll and a
frame hold one piece, a split two, and a set of tabs names each tab by its
piece's .title("a name"). my_window.menu(a_menu) puts a menu across the top; a
menu has .item(a_capsule, "Open") and .separator().

A text box is 25 characters wide unless its word says how wide: text_box("", 40)
is 40 characters wide, and text_box("", 100%) is as wide as the window it is
appended into, measured as it is appended -- 250% reaches past the window's
edge. A box given a percent goes straight into a window; in a row or a column
give it characters instead.

A window scrolls when a piece is appended past its edge: my_window.scroll.vertical(1)
and .scroll.horizontal(1) turn each way on, (0) off, and each read bare answers
which. A window starts as the rows arguments.scroll.vertical and
arguments.scroll.horizontal say, true unless config.ini or a line at the top of
the file says false. A console has the same two: satellite.help(satellite.console).

.text, .on, .value, .chosen, .path and .title read with no brackets and set with
them: .text("hi"), .on(1), .value(50), .chosen("red"). A progress bar's .value
is a percentage, .value(50%). There is no true to type yet, so .on takes 1 or 0.

A piece talks back by running a capsule, named bare and first: .pressed on a
button, .changed on anything a person can change, .clicked on anything but a
button or a menu; and on a window .closed, .key, .every(a_capsule, 1000) in
milliseconds, and .ask(a_capsule, "sure?") and .choose_a_file(a_capsule), whose
answer is its_window.answer. The capsule may declare nothing, the piece, or the
piece and its window. .press() is your program pressing a button itself.

A window also has .open(), .close(), .focus(), .resize(1024, 768),
.fullscreen(1) and .message("saved"). new puts a window up and goes straight on;
.open() waits until it has been drawn, and puts back a window that has closed:
its title, its size and its capsules come back, and what was in it closed with
it and is appended again. Every piece but a menu has .width, .height,
.colour("red") (or .color), .background("#222228") and .font("IBM Plex Mono",
12). A canvas draws with .line, .box, .circle, .arc and .write; .clear() and
.save("a.png").

satl refuses a wrong argument count, a method no piece has and a capsule nobody
wrote before a line runs, and a method this piece lacks (.resize on a button)
when its line runs. With no screen, the first window word stops with S730
NO_DISPLAY and satl exits 50.

example:

satellite.include(satellite)

satellite.capsule when_typed(satellite.variable.window the_box)
{
    satellite.console.display(the_box.text)
}

satellite.capsule when_done(satellite.variable.window the_button, satellite.variable.window its_window)
{
    its_window.close()
}

satellite.capsule satellite.main()
{
    satellite.variable.window my_window = satellite.window.new("a form", 400, 300)
    satellite.variable.window a_column = satellite.window.column()
    satellite.variable.window your_name = satellite.window.text_box("")
    your_name.changed(when_typed)
    satellite.variable.window done = satellite.window.button("done")
    done.pressed(when_done)
    a_column.append(your_name)
    a_column.append(done)
    my_window.append(a_column, 200, 150)
    satellite.return(satellite)
}

example, sections -- a file display in the top half, an input in the bottom:

satellite.include(satellite)

satellite.capsule satellite.main()
{
    satellite.variable.window main_window = satellite.window.new("window title", "version", 800, 600)
    main_window.section("section_name", 100%, 50%)
    main_window.section_name.widgets.displays.file("my_file_display", 100%, 100%)
    main_window.section("bottom", 100%, 50%)
    main_window.bottom.widgets.displays.input("folder")
    satellite.return(satellite)
}
