## 2. What was decided on the way

### 2.1 The engine: the 512-bit ask, asked again and refused again

The author's settlement diff recorded the engine as *"v1's `pcg32_k16384` with
its 32-bit output widened to 512, a typedef over `pcg_engines::ext_*`"*. The
tree's own dated investigation — `pcg/README.md`, 2026-08-27 — is why that
sentence cannot be built: **no such typedef exists to write** (the vendored
family's widest output is 64 bits), the `uint_x4` composition **compiles and
multiplies wrong**, and a real 512 means unpublished LCG constants and 1 MiB
of state per generator. Width is a property of the **sampler**, which already
answers at any digit count from 32-bit words. The engine is `pcg32_k16384`
exactly as it stands; PLAN's parenthetical now carries the ask *and* the
refusal so neither is lost; and the seam means the author overruling this with
constants in hand is one file to satisfy.

### 2.2 The seed: `getrandom(2)`, batched, and it cannot abort

v1 seeded through `std::random_device` — RDRAND under strace, never the
kernel, and a **throw with no catch** when RDRAND fails its hundred retries.
The replacement is a seed sequence over `getrandom(2)` flags 0, batched 1024
words per call because the table is 65,568 bytes and a syscall is not free;
ENOSYS falls back to `/dev/urandom`; and on a machine with neither the loop
**waits rather than inventing entropy from the clock**, because a weak seed
taken silently is DESIGN §1.1's "behind their back".

Measured on this machine, 2026-09-04: construction **~390 µs** against v1's
1.6 ms through `random_device` — and see §3.4 for why `strace` nearly hid it.

### 2.3 There is no watchdog, and the static_assert is the reason

v1 capped the spin at a drawn count in [500M, 600M] as a failsafe against a
clock stepped under a running deadline. The spin here runs against
`steady_clock` with `static_assert(is_steady)` on it — a deadline that cannot
be stepped needs no failsafe, so the cap v1 carried does not exist and clause
4's measurements are recorded **beside its absence** rather than beside a cap.

### 2.4 The sleep wakes on EINTR and only the flag decides what that meant

`clock_nanosleep(CLOCK_MONOTONIC, TIMER_ABSTIME)` — the absolute deadline
makes an EINTR resume free and driftless. SIGINT is installed without
`SA_RESTART` (M11, hard-won), so Ctrl-C wakes the sleep the moment it lands;
the handler's flag — reached through the same `Policy::interrupted` seam the
statement boundary reads — says whether the wake was a person, and any other
signal resumes the wait. An interrupted sleep answers the same nothing a
finished one does and the walk stops itself at the next boundary, exactly
DESIGN §10.2's contract. §4's transcript is the proof.

### 2.5 A bare spelling folds to its zero-argument shape — a rule, and M14 inherits it

The author's sentence — *"a bare tier spelling folds to the same number, and a
path is not a value"* — is implemented as a rule about spelling in
`cache::language_path()`: a **final** segment that names no word and no
matching shape folds to its `()` row when one exists. Interior segments do not
fold (the alias step has already taken `fast.range(...)` whole), so
`satellite.random.fast.foo` still stops under `random` with M5's suggestion
over the level that failed. **The consequence M14 inherits and must own**: a
bare `satellite.console.input` will mean `input()` `1 5 2` — a real read, not
a refusal — the day M14 installs that row. Said here so it is decided there
rather than discovered.

## 3. What building it found

### 3.1 The compiler had no whole-call road, and this was the milestone built to find it

`satellite.random.fast(2)` resolved perfectly — `satl --resolve` printed
`satellite.random.fast(digits)  1 7 4  walked` — and the evaluator's compiler
then refused its own resolved program as *"a method on this expression"*.
Resolve answers question ONE (*is the whole call a row?*) onto the **call
node**; the compiler read `info(target)` alone, which is where M10's `display`
and M11's methods carry their numbers — every path built before M13 numbered a
*word*, so the road for a number that includes its parentheses existed in
resolve, in the `.satc`, and nowhere in compile. One arm in
`compile_expressions.cpp` (`call()`, before the target road, absorbers
excluded) is the fix, and `input()` `1 5 2` at M14 walks through it for free.

### 3.2 The bare tier had no road either

Same shape one spelling over: `satellite.random.fast` bare stopped in the walk
with nothing to match, because the trie's children under `random` are all
shapes. §2.5's fold is the fix, in `language_path()` so the `.satc` writer and
resolve agree by construction.

### 3.3 Unbuilt module constants printed doubled backticks

Asking after the one reserved row this milestone leaves (`satellite.time.new`,
M29's) rendered ``` ``satellite.time.new`` ``` — the module-constant arm baked
backticks into the compiled text and S0721's row carries its own. The arm now
compiles the canonical spelling bare, like every other road. Cosmetic, found
only because a review went looking at its own boundary.

### 3.4 `strace` almost hid the seed, and the reason is worth keeping

Verifying clause 9 by `strace -e trace=getrandom` showed **2** kernel entries
where the seed sequence makes **18** `getrandom()` calls filling 16,392 words.
This machine's glibc 2.39 on kernel 6.12 serves `getrandom` through the
**vDSO** — per-thread ChaCha20 states the kernel seeds and manages — so the
bulk reads never cross into the kernel and a syscall trace under-counts by
design. The entropy is still the kernel's, there is still no RDRAND and no
throw path, and the instrumented count (a `Counting` seed sequence: 16,392
words requested) is the receipt `strace` could not be.
