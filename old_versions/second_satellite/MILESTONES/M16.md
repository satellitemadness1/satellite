## 1. What the milestone was

## 2. The decisions

### 2.1 A declaration does not construct, and the bare call shapes do

**The author's, 2026-09-05.** v1 default-constructed: `satellite.container.list
<...> l` was an empty list you could immediately append to, and `helpers.cpp`
argued for it — *"a declaration you cannot immediately .set() into would be
useless."* `prototype/M10` did the same.

**This tree cannot, because M12 already decided otherwise.** DESIGN §8.7,
settled 2026-09-03: *"Nothing is a state every type has. A declared variable of
any type holds nothing until a value is assigned to it."* A container exception
would not be a small one — it would make §8.7's sentence false for two of
thirteen rows, and the reader who trusted it would be wrong about exactly the
types where the difference bites.

So the numbering's own bare shapes were given their meaning:
`satellite.container.list()` `1 4 2 0` and `satellite.container.map()`
`1 4 1 0`, both carried since the 2026-08-28 transcription with nothing behind
them. A program writes

    satellite.container.list<satellite.variable.number> l = satellite.container.list()

and a bare declaration holds nothing, like every other type in the language.

**What it costs is one line per container a program builds, and what it buys is
that §8.7 stays true as written.** The overrule point is small and named: if
default construction is wanted later, it is one arm in `into_declared` and a
recorded correction to §8.7 — not a silent divergence.

### 2.2 A fold may land on the bare word it was spelled from

**The author's, 2026-09-05**, and it closes [MILESTONES/M7.md](M7.md) §6 item 1,
which had stood open since 2026-08-31 with M7 declining to pick because *"either
is a decision about the numbering."*

The problem: WORD_NUMBERS §2.2 assigns `sort()` `1 4 2 3` as *"ascending, no
key"* and `sort_up(key)` `1 4 2 7`, and there is **no `sort_up()`** — because
`sort()` already is it. So `my_list.sort("up")` named an option that exists at a
shape that does not, and answered S0523.

Two fixes existed. The author took the general one: **when the option is legal
and the folded shape does not exist at the written count, the fold retries the
bare word before refusing.** No alias row was minted;
[src/name_resolver/numbers.cpp](../src/name_resolver/numbers.cpp) is where the
rule runs and WORD_NUMBERS §1.5 carries it.

**The risk it takes on is written down beside it**, because a general rule can
be generally wrong: the fallback is only correct while a `<word>_<option>` row
whose shape is missing MEANS the bare word. That is true of `sort_up` because
§2.2 says so in as many words. A future word whose option CHANGES the meaning
must get its own shape row rather than lean on this.

### 2.3 Two numbers were minted: `search(pattern)`

**The author's, 2026-09-05.** v1 gives both containers a rich `.search(p)` — one
arm for both, a map per hit carrying the value, the key, the path and the score
— and the transcription carried no row for it. Asked whether M16 should ship the
subscript spelling alone or mint the number, the author answered that the first
satellite is the base and had already settled this: *"Let's add another number to
our list of numbers."*

`1 4 1 10` under the map and `1 4 2 26` under the list. **One word, two rows,
which is §1.5 and not a duplication** — a selector's number is reachable only
through the receiver's type, exactly as `size`, `empty` and `clear` already
appear under both. §2.2 went from 227 rows to 229; WORD_NUMBERS §2.6 records who
assigned them and why.

### 2.4 What `satellite.main`'s parameter cost, and the handover M17 needed

**One empty list, and PLAN was right that it was the whole of it.** The S0720
arm in `run_command.cpp` is gone; `main.parameters != 0` now binds
`Value::list(List{})` into slot 0 and hello world runs.

**But the value in the slot is not what the NAME reaches, and that is the
handover PLAN asked this milestone to write down rather than leave to be
discovered.** `arguments` under any of its six spellings is DESIGN §7.7's object
as far as resolve is concerned — names.cpp routes it there — so
`arguments.size()` is **S0532** naming what §7.7 holds, not a list method, and
that is true today and after M20 alike. Both halves are true at once and neither
is a stopgap: **the empty list is what M16 owed the slot, and the object is what
M20 owes the name.** A program that wants the list passes `arguments` on; a
program that wants the machine's answers waits for M20.

This is the M3/M4 failure caught before it happened instead of after, which is
what PLAN's M17 entry asked for in those words.

### 2.5 The walk keeps its own stack, and reads no dial

PLAN's M16 entry left this open in capitals — *"what this milestone owes instead
is one sentence about its own walk"* — with two acceptable answers: run on M9's
control stack and inherit its ceiling, or be a walker of its own and be bounded
by memory.

**It is a walker of its own, and there are four of them.** The search walk, the
comparator's container-against-container arm, `same()` over two containers, and
the printer. Every one keeps its stack on the heap, and none reads
`satellite.library.system.max_depth` — which is the 2026-09-01 reading being
obeyed rather than worked around: a ceiling in BYTES on the control stack is not
a count of levels a search can compare itself against, so **v1's `max_depth`
parameter and its "this structure nests deeper than" error are not ported.** A
search that cannot finish is a program that ran out of memory, and the language
has words and an exit status for that.

**Two of the four were not on anybody's list**, and finding them is the argument
for the rule being general rather than per-feature: `same()` became a walk the
moment two lists could be compared, and `text_of` became one the moment a list
could hold a list. Neither is "the search walk" that PLAN named.

### 2.6 What orders, and what a sort is

**Numbers (floats with them, by M15's rule that the two numeric arms compare as
values) and strings, by their stored codes. Nothing else, and a MIXED list is
refused** rather than ordered by a rule nobody chose.

The floor is the language's own — S0712 says `<` *"orders numbers, and nothing
else does"*. Strings are the one addition and are not a liberty:
`satellite.directory.list()` `1 18 4` answers a list of names that has to come
back sorted, so a language whose sort could not order strings would need a second
sort at M19. **The overrule point is an instant**: a list of
`satellite.variable.time` is plainly ordered and is refused here, because M13
gave an instant exactly one ability and its methods are M29's. One arm, the day
somebody wants it.

**Ties keep their place, because the sort is stable** — and that is worth a
sentence because QUAD's comparators carry an explicit tie-break for the opposite
reason. `std::sort` is not stable, so QUAD's programs had to add a second key to
stay deterministic; a stable sort makes that unnecessary rather than optional,
and a satellite program needs no tie-break field at all.

**Every sort mutates.** `l.sort()` orders the list it was asked of and the
answer is the receiver's new value, so `l.sort()` on its own line does what a
person writing it expects. `max` and `min` do not mutate — they answer an
element, not a list, which is why the write-back column is per row and not per
family.

### 2.7 `m[k] = v` is built, and v1 refused it

The one place this milestone overrules v1. Its `assign_index` answered *"a map is
written with .set(key, value)"* on the grounds that a second spelling of one
thing is a cost.

**DESIGN §12 lists `m[k] = v` and `l[i] = v` as ONE deferred entry and says
"fixing one fixes both"**, so a milestone that built the list half and declined
the map half would leave that entry half-struck — with no document saying which
half. The rule that makes them one is the storage-slot rule: assignment resolves
a slot, and the slot is the CONTAINER's in both cases.

**A map's string subscript is still a lookup and never a search**, which is v1's
rule kept exactly: *"a lookup that quietly became a search would change what
`m[k]` MEANS for every program that has ever read a map."* A missing key is
S0726 at every threshold, and `.search(pattern)` is how a map is asked the loose
question.

## 3. What building it found

### 3.1 The literal-option fold had never once run

M7 built WORD_NUMBERS §1.5's fold on 2026-08-31 and **nothing in the language
could reach it**: `sort` is the only word in the numbering with `<word>_<option>`
siblings, and there were no lists until this milestone. So the first time a
folded call was ever compiled was here — and it did not work.

The fold absorbs the literal into the NUMBER, so `my_list.sort("down")` **is**
`sort_down()` `1 4 2 5`, whose whole argument list is the receiver. The compiler
was still passing the string, so every folded call would have died on arity.
Fixed with a `folded_option` flag on `resolve::Info` and one `erase` in
`compile_expressions.cpp`.

**It needed a flag and could not be derived**, which is the part worth keeping:
an absorbed argument (`include(satellite)`) is recognisable from the numbering
alone, because the row's argument list names a reserved word. A fold is
recognisable only from the fact that the fold RAN — `sort("up")` lands on
`sort()`, whose spelling is the word the source already wrote, so nothing about
the resolved path can tell the compiler a string was consumed.

**This is PLAN M2's rule arriving four milestones late**: a mechanism gets a
consumer in the milestone that writes it, or it is not known to work. M7's own
entry said the fold's first real test would be M19's `satellite.file.open` modes.
It was M16's, and it failed.

### 3.2 A subscript's caret was on the bracket

An Index node's anchor token is its `[`, so the obvious `span_of(here())` drew
every containers refusal on punctuation — *"`[` was asked about position 9"*.
**This is M12's exact finding arriving at the second bracketing form the grammar
has**: M12 fixed `text_of` for Call, and Index and Slice were the same defect
waiting. Both are fixed — `text_of` walks through all three now, and the
subscript arms take their span from the subscript's own op.

### 3.3 A stale test binary reported `ok`

`make tests/eval_test/eval_test` was run with its output filtered to
`error|warning`, printed nothing, and the suite that ran afterwards was **the
binary from two days earlier**. It said `ok` over a section that had never been
compiled.

Recorded because [make_support/065-tests.mk](../make_support/065-tests.mk) opens
with this exact failure from the first satellite — *"`make test` ran binaries
nobody had built, reporting PASS from objects compiled before the change under
test. That is the worst failure a suite has: not a red line, a green one that is
out of date"* — and the fragment's own defences did not prevent it, because they
guard the *rules* and this was a **reader filtering the output**. The lesson is
not in the Makefile: check the binary's timestamp, or do not filter the build.

## 5. What is left open

1. **An instant does not order.** §2.6 — one arm, when M29 gives an instant its
   methods.
2. **`reserve(n)` buys almost nothing under copy-on-write.** The row exists
   because the numbering assigned it and it is honest — the next body reserves —
   but the mutation that follows copies anyway. DESIGN §12's in-place fast path
   is what would make it real, and that entry names its own condition.
3. **`.lines()` has no number, and PLAN says whose question that is.** M19's
   entry: *"whether it gets one is M16's question or M22's."* **This milestone
   declines it**, and the reason is that `.lines()` in v1 is a `ls`-style
   columnar rendering that reads the disk for each element — that is a REPL echo
   and belongs with the prompt, not with the list type. M22.
4. **The rich hit map's `path` is built and nothing consumes it yet.** It is
   exactly the input a write-through would need, which is why v1 built the read
   side that way; nothing in the language writes through a search result today.
5. **`satellite.container.arguments` `1 4 3` and `.result` `1 4 4` are not
   built**, and were never this milestone's — PLAN says so explicitly. M20 and
   M28.
