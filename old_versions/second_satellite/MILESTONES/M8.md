# M8 — `satellite.variable.number`: the port, and the sign it did not bring

**Landed 2026-08-31** (`d353522`).

DESIGN §8.1 is the specification, [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) §8's M8 entry is the
milestone and its done-when, [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) §6.1 is the survey of what was
being ported, and `src/satellite_number/` is what turned up. This is a **review
and not a plan** — what was decided on the way, what building it found, what it
left open, and which mutations prove its test.

`satl --number <a> <op> <b>` is the consumer. **Ten files — six sources under
four headers**, the same count v1 had, one new test of **208 check sites in seven
sections**, and one new block of three rows in `errors.def`.

**§6 WAS FOUR OPEN QUESTIONS AND IS NOW A RECORD OF CLOSING THEM**, on
2026-08-31, the same day the milestone landed. Three of the four turned out to
rest on the same mistake — a blocker named over a group of methods rather than
checked against any one of them — and the fourth turned out to be a limit rather
than a missing sentence. **The milestone grew two numbered paths, lost an error
row, and deleted a constant.** Every section below that the closing changed says
so where it changed, and §6 is the whole of the argument.

---

## 1. What the milestone was

**A port, plus the one thing the port does not bring with it.** PLAN §6.1
surveyed the first satellite's `satellite_number` at 10 files and 1509 lines,
internally closed, every file already under §3's target, and concluded it *"very
nearly ports as-is."* That was accurate. The part that is not a port is the sign:

> DESIGN §8.1: *"A number's sign is an explicit `satellite.variable.bool` named
> `positive`, defaulting to `true` — a number with no sign written is positive,
> and something has to flip the flag for it to be otherwise. The magnitude never
> carries a sign of its own."*

The first satellite does the opposite. Its `Number` packs the sign into the
significand, and when the magnitude is boxed the significand holds `1` or `-1`
instead of a value — with a paragraph in `bignum_number.hpp` warning that a
caller reading it without checking would see every large number as 1, *"silently,
and only for numbers big enough that a test with small inputs would never show
it."*

**It is deliberately the same mechanism DESIGN §8.6 gives a float**, which is the
whole reason this is a milestone seven positions ahead of M15 rather than half of
it: negation, `abs`, the sign of a product and the ordering of negatives are
written once here and are true of both types.

## 3. What was decided on the way

### 3.1 `sizeof` was checked first, as PLAN §6.1 asked, and it decided the shape

§6.1 calls `sizeof(Number)` *"the one number that could make this port not fit,
and it is cheap to check first."* It was, and it does fit — but the measurement
answered a second question nobody had asked. Taken 2026-08-31 before a line was
written:

    v1 as it stands   (sig, exp, ptr)                 32
    flat, with an explicit bool positive_             32     Value comes out 40
    a separate Magnitude type, Number wraps it        40     Value comes out 48

**The tempting refactor is the one this ruled out.** DESIGN §8.6 corrects an
earlier draft of the float for storing the sign twice and says the fix is *"one
sign, held once"* — which reads as an instruction to cut a `Magnitude` type out
of `Number` so that a float can hold two of them under one flag. It costs eight
bytes: a nested struct's tail padding is not reusable by the type holding it, so
the bool that fits free in the hole after `int exp_` needs its own eight-byte
slot the moment the other three fields become a member.

So the fields are flat and the order is load-bearing, and both the header and
`bignum.hpp` say so rather than leaving a future edit to rediscover it. **M15
inherits the sign rule and does not inherit the trick**, which its entry in PLAN
§8 now says.

### 3.2 "There is no negative zero" became structural rather than checked

DESIGN §8.6 invariant 3 says to *"normalise it away on construction, not at
comparison time."* The port went one step further, and it fell out of the sign
change rather than being designed: `make()` returns a default-constructed
`Number` the moment the magnitude is zero, and a default `Number` is positive
because the flag defaults to `true`. **There is no path in the module that can
build a negative zero**, so nothing checks for one.

**And that is a difference from the float, which is worth knowing before M15.**
§8.6 warns that a zero-initialised float struct reads as negative zero and calls
it *"a footgun and also a check."* A zero-initialised `Number` is the number 0.
The asymmetry is real: the float's `positive` has to be set by construction
because its two halves can be zero independently, and the number's does not.

The test does not take the structure on trust. [sign.cpp](../tests/number_test/sign.cpp)
enumerates **twenty-two** ways to reach a zero — every operator, both signs, the
small form and the boxed one, `ceil(-0.5)`, `round(-0.4)`, `from_small(false, 0,
3)` — and asserts five things about each. A list and not a loop over operations,
so a path added later is a line added there rather than a case nobody noticed was
missing.

### 3.3 The sign cost one comparison and paid for four things

DESIGN §8.6 names the cost: *"the opposite-sign case is where the code is, and
that is the one cost of sign-magnitude — a comparison and a conditional swap that
the signed-halves draft got for free."* True, and it is the smaller half of the
ledger.

- **`LLONG_MIN` stops being a special case.** v1 carried three separate blocks —
  in the constructor, in `from_small()` and in `magnitude()` — because
  `-LLONG_MIN` has no `long long` to land in, so *"`sig_` can be negated"* was an
  invariant every other operation leaned on. An unsigned magnitude holds 2⁶³ and
  the whole argument dissolves. **`number_core.cpp` came out 15 code lines
  shorter than v1's**, which is where all three blocks were.
- **And it fixes a bug that shipped in v1.** `Number(LLONG_MIN).to_integer()`
  returns **false** there — `BigInt::fits_ll()` caps the magnitude at `LLONG_MAX`,
  so the one value whose magnitude does not fit a signed integer is refused by
  the same class whose constructor had just accepted it and whose `to_string()`
  prints it correctly. Measured against v1's own objects before the claim was
  written down; `sign.cpp` asserts the round trip both through the constructor
  and through `parse()`.
- **The opposite-sign addition can no longer overflow at all.** Subtracting the
  smaller unsigned magnitude from the larger always fits, so that branch never
  falls through to the `BigInt` path — where v1's signed add fell through
  whenever the sum left the range. **More additions are allocation-free after
  this change than before it.**
- **The small form got wider, and a failing fixture is what found it.** The
  significand's ceiling moved from `LLONG_MAX` to `ULLONG_MAX`, so
  `9e18 + 9e18` stays inline where v1 would have boxed it. The first version of
  [arithmetic.cpp](../tests/number_test/arithmetic.cpp) used exactly that sum as
  its *"this one overflows"* fixture, on the assumption that the port had not
  changed the range, and it failed. The assertion in the tree now says what
  actually happens and why.

`bignum_internal.hpp` gets the same dividend one size down: `POW10` runs to 10¹⁹
rather than v1's 10¹⁸, because that is the largest power of ten an unsigned
significand holds — one more scale that stays out of the allocator.

**Counted, because "the sign made it smaller" is the kind of claim that is
pleasant and usually wrong.** Code lines, comments and blanks excluded, v1
against this port:

    bignum.hpp              8 →   3    -5    the size note moved out of it
    bignum_bigint.hpp      35 →  32    -3    Bits32 is satellite_random's
    bignum_internal.hpp    29 →  32    +3    one more power of ten
    bignum_number.hpp      79 →  81    +2    two shift declarations, less the
                                             deleted division ceiling
    limbs.cpp             224 → 224     0
    number_core.cpp       140 → 125   -15    the three LLONG_MIN blocks
    number_query.cpp      128 → 155   +27
    number_arith.cpp      152 → 177   +25    the two shifts and the power_of
                                             they share, less the two clamp
                                             lines §6.1 deleted
    render.cpp             38 →  38     0
    random.cpp             62 →  62     0
                          --- ---   ---
                          895 → 929   +34

**So the module is thirty-four code lines LARGER, and two files are why.**
`number_query.cpp`'s +27 is fifteen lines of `max`, `min` and `clamp` — three
methods v1 does not have at all (§4.2) — plus `to_integer()` absorbing the signed
conversion the deleted blocks used to do three times over. `number_arith.cpp`'s
+25 is twenty-seven lines of `shift_left`, `shift_right` and their shared
`power_of`, which v1 also does not have (§4.1), less the two lines of clamp §6.1
deleted.

**Net of the forty-two lines v1 has no equivalent for, the port is 887 against
895 — eight lines smaller.** That is the honest form of the claim, and it has now
moved twice: it read *"the module came out shorter"* before anything was counted,
then *"−9 net of the three methods v1 never had"* once it was, and it is −8 net of
five. The sign change paid for itself both times; what changed is how many things
the milestone spent the winnings on.

### 3.4 `BigInt::fits_ll()` was the class's only signedness opinion, and it is gone

`BigInt` was already unsigned in v1 — `std::vector<unsigned> limbs_` — and the
one place it knew anything about signed integers was `fits_ll()`/`to_ll()`, which
asked whether a magnitude fits a **`long long`**. That existed because v1's
`Number` met its boxed form at a signed significand. With the sign out of the
magnitude the honest question is whether it fits the small form's significand, so
the pair became `fits_u64()`/`to_u64()` and the conversion to a signed integer
moved up one layer to `Number::to_integer()`, where the sign lives. **That is
where §3.3's `LLONG_MIN` fix physically is**, and it is one function rather than
three.

### 3.5 The module stopped being internally closed, on purpose

PLAN §6.1 and `SCRATCH.md/PORTING.md` §2 both say `satellite_number` *"ports
alone"* — every include a sibling or the standard library. It does not any more,
by one line, and the line is `#include "satellite_random/random.hpp"`.

v1 carried its own `Bits32` (in `bignum_bigint.hpp`) and its own
`MAX_RANDOM_DIGITS` (on `Number`). **This tree already has both**, in
`satellite_random/random.hpp`, which landed at M2 ahead of any milestone that
calls it — same 100,000, same argument, same seam. Copying v1's would put two
facts in two places each, and FORMAT/CXX.md §1's second rule is what that
violates. The failure it prevents is the one that registry's own note describes:
two ceilings that agree until somebody edits one.

**It costs nothing else**, and that is not luck. `random.hpp` deliberately names
no PCG type — *"so that nothing above this line includes an Apache-2.0 header"* —
which `pcg/README.md` calls worth one pointer indirection. This is that decision
collecting, and it is its first consumer.

### 3.6 The dial got a meaning, and M6 had left the hole in the right shape

`machine_limits/limits.hpp` says of an unset dial: *"three of the four have no
reader at M6 and inventing a default for them would be inventing their meaning …
the milestone that will read it decides what no answer means — v1's
`read_division_digits()` is exactly that decision, made where the division is."*

M8 is that milestone for `satellite.library.system.division_digits` `1 14 2 1`,
and the decision is one function: `limits::division_digits()`, **unset means 34**.

**It is beside the dial and not inside `Number`, which is the seam turned the
other way up from v1.** v1 kept a `DEFAULT_DIVISION_DIGITS` on its bignum class
because its evaluator read the library namespace itself; here the namespace *is*
`machine_limits`, so 34 and its justification live there and `Number::divide`
takes a count and never invents one. What stayed on `Number` is
`kMaxDivisionDigits`, because a ceiling on what a division will spend is a fact
about the division.

**`SCRATCH.md/PORTING.md` §6 predicted this row wrong and it is worth saying so.**
It settled on *"a compile-time default at M8, and the library lookup restored at
the milestone that gives the namespace values"* — written on 2026-08-27, when the
registry could name the question and not answer it. M6 built the storage on
2026-08-30. The row's reasoning was right and its conclusion was one milestone out
of date by the time anyone acted on it.

### 3.7 The consumer is three operands and not an expression

PLAN §8's opening rule is that a milestone is *"a thing that works and can be
demonstrated"*, and M6 and M7 each shipped a command for it. Nothing executes
until M10, so without one M8 lands as a module with a test binary.

The obvious command reads `satl --number "(1+2)*3"` — **and it needs a grammar to
do it, and DESIGN §6 is already the grammar.** A second one here would be a
second place expression syntax is decided, which is the failure
`satellite_cache/paths.hpp` opens by warning about and `format.def`'s rule
forbids. Two literals and an operator is an *operand form*, the same shape
`satl --limits <file>` already has: it cannot drift from §6 because it is not
trying to be §6, and when M9 compiles expressions the whole question moves there.

**It builds a source line out of its own arguments**, which is how M5's reporter
works over a command line at all: joining the three operands with single spaces
produces one line whose byte offsets are known while it is being built, so
`satl --number 1 / 0` draws a caret under the `0` exactly the way `satl --check`
draws one under a token.

### 3.8 S06xx was taken, and it is the second reservation honoured end to end

`errors.def` reserved S06xx for *"numbers, DESIGN §8.1, M8"* back at M5. Four
rows landed in it and nothing moved to make room. `tests/reporter_test/codes.cpp`
had a loop asserting that S06xx and S07xx are both empty; it now asserts S07xx
alone, which is a one-number edit and is the mechanism working — the same edit M7
made to the same loop.

**A FOURTH ROW WAS ADDED AND RETIRED THE SAME DAY, AND THE GAP AT 612 IS THE
MECHANISM WORKING.** S0612 answered `satl --number 7 % 2` with *"`%` is not built
yet — `satellite.variable.number.modulus` is `1 6 4 12`, and PLAN.md §8 puts it at
M15 with the rounding rule it needs"*, and exited 3 rather than 1. **The sentence
was wrong.** DESIGN §8.6 specifies modulus in full and files it under *exact and
bounded, never rounds*, so nothing was missing but the reading — §6 has how that
was found. `%` computes now, S0611's list is five operators rather than four, and
the row is **deleted rather than repurposed**: this file's header says a code
*"has to survive a row being deleted"*, and a number that meant two things at two
dates is the one property that does not survive.

**It is the second row this registry has retired and not the first.** S0501
`RESOLVE_TOO_DEEP` went earlier the same day, because DESIGN §7.5 says the
language has no depth limit; this one goes because DESIGN §8.6 had already
specified the operation the row apologised for. **Both rows were deleted for
disagreeing with a document that was written before them**, which is the same
failure §6.5 is about, arriving twice in one day in two different blocks.

**Deleting a row is invisible to every `static_assert` in `codes.hpp`**, which is
why `tests/reporter_test/codes.cpp` opens with a count of the table and why that
count now reads 61 rather than 62. The table still ascends and the switch still
compiles over a shorter list; the assertion is the only thing in the build that
notices, and its message says to record the change in MILESTONES, which is this
paragraph.

**There is no overflow row, and that is DESIGN §7.5 rather than an omission.**
The language has no limits: a number's size is bounded by memory the way a list's
length is, so there is no width at which addition refuses. What ends a runaway is
M6's watchdog at `MEMORY_MAX`, in words and with exit 4.

## 4. What building it found

### 4.1 Two of the eleven numbered paths had no meaning to build, for half a day

PLAN §8's M8 entry says *"`shift_left` `1 6 4 1` through `round` `1 6 4 9`, and
`shift_right(n)` `1 6 4 11`"* land here. **The first satellite has neither shift,
and no document defined either.** They enter the language through DESIGN §5.5, as
an aside that bit shifts *"if ever needed"* would be spelled
`satellite.variable.number.shift_left(n)` — which is an argument about where a
path hangs, made while correcting a slip about a top-level `satellite.number`, and
not a specification of an operation.

So both were moved to M15, on the argument that *"a bit shift on an exact base-10⁹
decimal does not have one obvious meaning"*: ×2ⁿ is a true bit shift and
terminates, because 2 divides 10; ×10ⁿ is one line, being an adjustment to the
exponent, and makes the name a lie; and neither says what happens to a receiver
with a fractional part.

**THAT WAS THE RIGHT FINDING AND THE WRONG CONCLUSION, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS
WHERE §6 STARTS.** The finding is that nothing specified the operation. The
conclusion treated "undecided" as "blocked", and moved it to the milestone that
owns a *different* undecided thing — the rounding rule — without checking whether
this one needed it. It did not. **DESIGN §5.5 now specifies both**, decided
2026-08-31:

> `a.shift_left(n)` is `a × 2ⁿ` and `a.shift_right(n)` is `a ÷ 2ⁿ`. Both are
> exact, neither ever rounds, and a fractional receiver is not a special case.

**The third objection answers the other two.** `2⁻ⁿ` has an exact decimal
expansion of exactly *n* places, because 2 divides 10 — `1 ÷ 2¹⁰` is
`0.0009765625` and not an approximation of it — so `shift_right` terminates as
surely as `shift_left`, reads no digit budget, and belongs in DESIGN §8.6's first
class beside it: *exact and bounded, never rounds*. A receiver with a fraction
then needs no rule at all, because a multiplication does not care: `7.5` shifted
left once is exactly `15` and right once is exactly `3.75`. **The question that
looked like it needed a decision about rounding turned out to need a fact about
the number 10.**

`number_arith.cpp` does not divide in either direction. `a ÷ 2ⁿ` is
`a × 5ⁿ ÷ 10ⁿ`, and dividing by a power of ten is an adjustment to `exp_` — so
the direction that could in principle not terminate never runs a long division at
all, and the two are one multiplication each over a `power_of()` built by
squaring. **Twelve lines of code behind a day of the milestone's argument**, which
is the honest ratio and is worth writing down.

### 4.2 Three of the nine were written, not ported

v1's number method surface is `abs`, `ceil`, `floor`, `round`, `to_string`,
`digits` and `negate`, plus the arithmetic as methods. It has **no `max`, `min`
or `clamp`**. They are one comparison each over `compare()`, so the cost is
nothing — but *"ten of its fourteen methods land here"* reads as ten things being
carried across, and five were.

They return **by value and not by `const Number &`**, which is the one place the
module refuses to copy the standard library: a reference-returning `max` binds
happily to a temporary and dangles at the end of the full expression. It costs one
refcount, and only when the magnitude is boxed. `clamp` with `low` above `high`
answers `low`, deterministically, written down rather than left undefined the way
`std::clamp` leaves it — the alternative is refusing, which needs a span the
caller has and this layer does not.

### 4.3 `.digits()` had no number — the mirror image of §4.1

v1 exposes `.digits()`, and `digit_count()` carries a whole paragraph on why it is
not `.length()`: *"one word means one thing; `length` means how many items on a
string, a list and a map, and a number holds no items."* **WORD_NUMBERS §2.2 had
no row for it under `1 6 4`.** So the C++ method was here, tested, printed by
`satl --number` on every run — and the language could not call it.

**The mirror image is exact and worth stating as a pair.** The two shifts had
numbers and no meaning; `digits` had a meaning and no number. Both are ways for a
registry and an implementation to disagree, and only one of them is visible to a
compiler: a path with nothing behind it fails no build, and a method with no path
fails no build either. What found both was writing this review.

**`1 6 4 15` was appended on the author's decision, 2026-08-31.** §1.2 is *never
renumber, never reuse*, and appending is neither: nothing moved and no
already-written program changed meaning. §2.2 goes to **223 rows and 220 distinct
numbers**, `words.def` to 217 `SAT_NUMBERED` rows and 255 nodes, and
`tests/words_test` carries all three counts — the only three assertions in the
build that can tell an appended row from a dropped one.

### 4.4 The 2:1 skew is one level in from where the comment points

`random.cpp` records the first satellite's measurement of what a bare `%` costs:
*"1398410 / 904307 / 697283 against the 998574 / 1001152 / 1000274 this path
gives — a 2:1 skew."* PLAN §9 says measure on this machine rather than quote, so
`tests/number_test/draw.cpp` re-runs it — and the first attempt could not
reproduce it, which is what found the real mechanism.

**`draw_below()`'s rejection is against a raw 32-bit `%`, and for a bound of 3
that bias is one extra preimage in 4.29 billion.** No test sees that. The visible
2:1 is the **second** rejection, in `BigInt::random_below()`: the top limb is
drawn over `[0, top+1)` — which is `[0, 4)` for a bound of 3 — and folding that
back with `% bound` instead of redrawing maps 0,1,2,3 to 0,1,2,0. The section
runs both over three million draws from a splitmix32 stub and asserts that one is
flat and the other is not.

**Driven by a stub and not by PCG, deliberately.** `random.hpp` names that shape
itself: *"the first satellite proved this seam is generator-agnostic by driving it
with a home-grown splitmix32 stub in its tests."* What is measured is the
sampler, not the generator.

### 4.5 `satellite_random` has a consumer, and the sentence narrows rather than ending

LAYOUT.md has called it *"the one module in the tree with no consumer"* since M2,
and 040-sources.mk explained by naming this milestone. `tests/number_test`
links it. **`satl` still does not** — nothing in the interpreter draws a number,
because `satellite.random.*` reaches no milestone at all. So what changed is that
the module is exercised rather than only compiled, and both documents now say the
narrower thing.

## 5. Half a decision was already made, by porting

`Number::divide` rounds **half-up**: it computes one guard digit past the budget
and bumps when that digit is `5` or more, which is why `satl --number 2 / 3` ends
in a `7`. That is v1's behaviour, ported unchanged and not chosen.

DESIGN §8.6's *"Still open"* is the rounding rule — truncate, half-up, or
half-even — and PLAN §8 makes it M15's blocker. **The language already rounds one
way at one operation**, and that is not the same as the rule being chosen: M15's
question is what the float does and whether the number's division should agree.
The honest position is that a default arrived by porting. It is written into
PLAN's M15 entry so that milestone inherits the fact rather than the surprise,
and `tests/number_test/arithmetic.cpp` asserts the current behaviour so that
changing it is a visible edit.

## 6. What it left open, and what closing it took

**This section listed four open things when the milestone landed and all four
were closed the same day**, 2026-08-31, on the author's decision. It is kept as a
record of the closing rather than replaced by the answers, because three of the
four failed for one reason and that reason is the finding.

### 6.1 The division clamp was silent, and the fix was to delete the clamp

> *As written:* `Number::divide` caps a count at 10,000, so a
> `satellite_config.ini` setting `division_digits=50000` gets 10,000 and nothing
> says so. DESIGN §1.1 forbids doing anything behind a program's back. It is one
> row in the S06xx block and a caller with a span — the config reader has one,
> this layer does not, and **M11 is where a program can ask.**

**Every sentence of that is true and it is an answer to the wrong question.** It
reads the fault as a missing sentence, and the missing sentence was about a
constant that DESIGN §7.5 does not allow to exist: *"no recursion ceiling, no
maximum nesting depth, no constant in a header deciding how big a thing the user
may write."* `kMaxDivisionDigits = 10000` is the third of those exactly. Adding an
error row would have been satl announcing a limit the language does not have.

**So it is gone.** `division_digits=50000` is fifty thousand digits and satl
computes them — measured on this tree, from a real config file, and the answer is
50,000 digits long. What ends a runaway is M6's watchdog at `MEMORY_MAX`, in words
and with exit 4, which is **the same answer §3.8's block note already gives for
why there is no overflow row**: a number's size is bounded by memory the way a
list's length is, and a division to N digits is a number of size N. That sentence
was one screen above the clamp for the whole milestone.

**What the FILE may say is bounded, and that is not the same claim.** A count
below one keeps nothing; a count satl cannot hold in a machine word cannot be
acted on. Both are refused at the line of the config that wrote them, with a caret
under the value — S0807 and S0808, rows M6 already had, through a one-row-per-dial
table in `config_internal.hpp` so that the second dial to be given a meaning is a
line and not a branch. **A bound on what a configuration file may contain is not a
bound on the language**, and every other setting in that module has had one since
M6.

**And closing it found two more silent substitutions nobody had reported.**
`limits::division_digits()` turned a dial of 0 into 34 and anything past
`UINT_MAX` into `UINT_MAX`, both without a word, both for the same reason the
clamp existed: the function had no way to point at the file, so it guessed. Now
the file is refused and the function is a lookup with nothing left in it.

**M11 still owns the half this could not reach** — a *program* retuning the dial
at run time — and it inherits a function with no policy in it rather than one with
two.

### 6.2 `.digits()` had no number

Closed by appending `1 6 4 15`; §4.3 has it, and it is the one of the four that
was exactly as small as it looked.

### 6.3 What the shifts mean

Closed in DESIGN §5.5: a shift is `× 2ⁿ` and `÷ 2ⁿ`, both exact, both terminating
because 2 divides 10, and a fractional receiver is not a special case. §4.1 has
the argument and why the original conclusion was wrong. **They came back from M15
and landed here.**

### 6.4 `Number::modulo` worked while `1 6 4 12` did not exist

> *As written:* The C++ is ported and tested; the path is M15's. S0612 makes the
> gap a sentence rather than a silence, which is the best this milestone can do,
> but a function nothing may call is a thing to notice rather than to leave.

**The gap was not there.** DESIGN §8.6 has a subsection headed *"Modulus, and why
it is exact"* which specifies the operation completely — `a − b × trunc(a/b)`,
truncated and not floored so the result takes the sign of the dividend, with
`7.5 % 2.1 = 1.2` worked out line by line — and its classification puts `%` in
**class 1: exact and bounded, never rounds.** The section says so in the sentence
that introduces it: *"it never rounds — which is worth stating because `/` always
does."*

So `satl --number 7 % 2` answers 1, `7.5 % 2.1` answers exactly `1.2`, and S0612
is deleted. `Number::modulo` was never a function nothing may call; it was a
function nothing had checked the specification for.

### 6.5 THE ONE FINDING BEHIND THREE OF THE FOUR

**A blocker named over a GROUP cannot be checked against any one member of it.**
PLAN §8's M8 entry defers four methods in one sentence — *"`power(a, b)`,
`modulus(a, b)`, `truncate(a)` and `sqrt(a)` are M15's, because none of them can
be finished before the rounding rule is chosen"* — and that sentence is true of
`power` and `sqrt`, false of `modulus`, and beside the point for `truncate`, which
waits on the float rather than on the rule. Then the two shifts were added to the
same group, on the reasoning that their open question was *the same kind* as the
rounding rule, and DESIGN §8.6's own classification already said they were not.

**Nothing in the build could have caught it, and one document contradicted it in
print.** A deferred path costs no compile error and no failing test; it is a
sentence in a plan agreeing with a sentence in an error row. DESIGN §8.6 is the
document that disagreed, and it had disagreed since before the milestone started.

**This is MILESTONES/M7.md §7.1's finding in a third form**, after `member()` and
`call()` being two refusal sites and the cache counts being right in total while
half the mechanism was disabled — and after §8 below found it inside `compare()`
during this same milestone. Each time the shape is one claim covering several
things, holding for the group, and not checked against the members. **The first
three were about tests; this one is about a plan, which is the version no test
can find.**

**What it cost to close all four: about forty lines of code.** Twelve for the two
shifts, a deleted constant and two deleted branches for the clamp, one line in
`words.def`, one arm in `number_command.cpp`, and a seventeen-line range check in
the config reader. **What it cost to find: reading DESIGN §8.6 to the end.**

## 7. What it cost to start

PLAN §9 asks for a re-measurement every milestone, and `make startup` took this
one — the second time the harness has been used rather than a person.
**2026-08-31, load 0.88, STATIC=full**, against startup.rows' M7 baseline:

    bare int main(){return 0;}       0.555 ms   (M7: 0.546)
    satl (opening information)       0.754 ms   share 0.199  (M7: 0.171)
    satl --version                   0.759 ms   share 0.204  (M7: 0.186)
    satl --words                     0.945 ms   share 0.390  (M7: 0.354)
    satl --tokens hello_world.satl   0.856 ms   share 0.301  (M7: 0.271)
    satl --tokens class_test.satl    1.154 ms   share 0.599  (M7: 0.573)
    satl --limits                    1.493 ms   share 0.938  (M7: 0.909)
    satl --resolve hello_world.satl  0.893 ms   share 0.338  (M7: 0.308)
    satl --resolve frames.satl       1.147 ms   share 0.592  (M7: 0.565)
    satl --number 1 + 1              0.769 ms   share 0.214  (new at M8)
    satl --number 1 / 3              0.786 ms   share 0.231  (new at M8)

**SATL'S OWN SHARE IS 0.204 ms AGAINST M7's 0.186, AND IT IS THE IMAGE — WITH
ONE PIECE OF EVIDENCE M7's ATTRIBUTION DID NOT HAVE.** Every row moved by about
the same amount, and one of the rows that moved is the **bare `satl`**, which
prints its opening information and never reaches an arm at all. A cost paid
before a command is chosen cannot be that command's arithmetic. What changed is
the size of the image: satl at STATIC=full went from **1,828,776 bytes to
1,887,016**, which is +58,240 for six `satellite_number` objects and
`number_command.o`.

**M8's arithmetic is under the run-to-run noise, which is what the two new rows
establish.** `--number 1 + 1` is 0.010 ms over `--version`: two operands read,
one add with an overflow check, five lines printed. `1 / 3` is 0.017 ms over
that, for long division to 34 significant digits and the one allocation the
answer boxes into. **The harness was run three times on this build and the spread
between runs was 0.027 ms — larger than either figure** — so those two rows are a
bound and not a measurement, and they are recorded as one.

**§6'S FOLLOW-UP PASS WAS NOT RE-MEASURED, AND THAT IS A GAP RATHER THAN A
RESULT.** It added a `%` arm, two shift functions and a range check, and
deleted a constant — small against the 58 KiB of objects above, and unmeasured
because the machine was under load 2.8 when the pass finished and a row taken
there is not comparable to one taken at 0.88. `make startup` is one command and
startup.rows takes a new row in one line; the next milestone's measurement will
carry both.

**DESIGN §8.2's claim is checked where it can be checked exactly.** *"Small exact
integers, `bool` and nil must never allocate"* is a claim about allocation, not
about time, so `tests/number_test` asserts `payload_bytes()` — a thousand
increments reach 1000 having allocated nothing, `ULLONG_MAX` still fits inline,
and one past it boxes. A clock could not have said any of that at this size.

decision and not merely about the arithmetic.

### The three findings

**THE FIRST IS THAT `compare()` HAS THREE REVERSAL SITES AND TWO OF THEM RESTED
ON ONE ASSERTION EACH.** The first time this table was run, deleting `sa < 0 ? -c
: c` from the general path failed **one** check, and from the scaled small path
**one**. Six negative-ordering assertions all went through the hot path — both
operands small, one scale — and the other two mechanisms were being asserted by
accident. `sign.cpp` now has fixtures per mechanism, and the same mutation fails
six each. **This is the finding MILESTONES/M7.md §7.1 records twice — once about
`member()` and `call()` being two refusal sites, and once about the cache counts
being right in total while half the mechanism was disabled — arriving a third
time**: a claim asserted over a whole behaviour cannot see which of several code
paths produced it, and the fixtures that happen to exist decide which path gets
covered.

**THE SECOND IS THE ONE ROW WITH NO NUMBER, AND IT IS HONEST RATHER THAN
MISSING.** Putting `MAX_POW10` back to 18 does not fail a test — it fails the
build, because `bignum_internal.hpp` carries a `static_assert` that the constant
and the table agree. The nineteenth power of ten is a **performance** property:
one more scale that stays out of the allocator, worth nothing to correctness. The
failure that *would* corrupt a value is the constant and the array disagreeing,
and that is what is checked, at compile time, which is FORMAT/CXX.md §1's third
rule — *a fact anything breaks on has to be somewhere a `static_assert` can see
it.*

**THE THIRD IS THE ZERO-COUNT GUARD, WHICH FAILED NOTHING ON ITS FIRST RUN.**
`divide()` refuses a count of zero the way it refuses a zero divisor, and
deleting that refusal failed **no assertion at all** — because the fixture beside
it was `1 / 3`, which reaches the rounding branch, resizes its digits to none and
comes back as zero by arithmetic rather than by rule. **`1 / 2` does not reach
that branch**: it terminates, so without the guard it answers `0.5` to a caller
who asked for no digits, and the two zero-budget divisions disagree with each
other. One fixture was asserting a behaviour the code produced for an unrelated
reason. Adding `1 / 2` makes the mutation fail, which is the assertion arriving
the right way round — and it is §6.5's finding again at the smallest possible
scale: a claim checked against one member of a set it is supposed to cover.

### The one that matters most

**`compare()` failing to reverse inside the negative half.** It gets every
positive pair right. A suite whose fixtures were all positive would report PASS
over a type that sorts half its values backwards — and DESIGN §8.6 names the
consequence, because QUAD's seven sort comparators are all `if (a != b) return a
> b`. That is why `sign.cpp` is the largest section, why every claim in
`rounding.cpp` is made twice, once each side of zero, and why the three reversal
sites are now asserted separately.

**And the fold measurement is worth keeping.** `random_below` folding instead of
redrawing gives **1500806 / 748957 / 750237** over three million draws — the 2:1
the first satellite measured, reproduced on this tree, at the site §4.4 says it
actually lives.
